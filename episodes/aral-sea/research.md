## R2 The sea before (1960) and the cause
- 1960: about 68,000 km2, 1,100 km3, the world's fourth-largest lake; surface 53.4 m (Wikipedia, citing sources).
- Fed by the Amu Darya and Syr Darya; the Amu Darya supplied about 70 % of its water (Global Voices 2026).
- 1960s: Soviet planners diverted both rivers to irrigate cotton and other crops in Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and
  Turkmenistan (NASA Earth Observatory, World of Change). By 1960 20-60 km3 a year already went to the land.
- Level fell ~20 cm/yr in the 1960s, 50-60 cm/yr in the 1970s, 80-90 cm/yr in the 1980s; salinity rose from about
  10 g/L to about 30 g/L by 1990 (seawater ~35) (Wikipedia).
- Qaraqum (Karakum) Canal: 30-75 % of its water lost to leaks and evaporation (Wikipedia) - use as "much of it".
- Fishing: about 40,000 jobs; reportedly one-sixth of the Soviet Union's fish catch (Wikipedia); annual catch
  80,000 t until the 1960s (Global Voices). Commercial fishing ended by 1987 (Wikipedia).

## R3 The collapse
- 1987: the sea split into the North (Small) and South (Large) Aral (Wikipedia; Times of Central Asia).
- 1960-1998: area -60 %, volume -80 %; 1998: 28,687 km2 (Wikipedia). 2004: 17,160 km2, ~25 % (Wikipedia).
- 2014: "the eastern lobe of the South Aral Sea completely disappeared" for the first time in modern history
  (NASA Earth Observatory); the dry bed is now called the Aralkum Desert. Levels fluctuate with wet and dry years
  2009-2018 (NASA).
- Muynak (Moynaq), Uzbekistan: former fishing port with canneries; the shore is now on the order of 100 km away
  (travel/press sources give 90-130 km) (attr); the ship graveyard holds about a dozen hulks today (attr).
- Aralsk, Kazakhstan: the sea retreated about 100 km south of the town before the dam (Wikipedia); 40 km when the
  dam was finished (Global Voices) - say "dozens of kilometres".
- Dust: storms lift tens of millions of tonnes of salt and dust from the dry bed each year; sources give 80 million
  (Global Voices 2026) to over 100 million t (Times of Central Asia) -> "tens of millions of tonnes".
- Health (attr, Wikipedia citing studies): at least 4.4 million people affected; high rates of respiratory
  illness and anaemia reported; child mortality 75 per 1,000 (2009). Use sparingly, attributed.
- Vozrozhdeniya (Rebirth) Island: Soviet open-air biological weapons test site; anthrax buried there in 1988
  (estimates 100-200 t); the island joined the mainland around 2001 as the sea shrank (sources say 2001 or 2002 ->
  "around 2001"); a US-funded cleanup (up to about $6 million) dug up and decontaminated 11 burial pits, 2001-2002
  (Arms Control Association; DTRA). Kantubek: abandoned town on the island (Commons photo 1994).

## R4 The North Aral comeback (Kazakhstan)
- Kokaral dam (Dike Kokaral): announced Oct 2003, finished Aug 2005, 13 km long; about $86 million, with a
  $64.5 million World Bank loan (Eurasianet; Wikipedia). It blocks the North Aral's outflow into the south.
- The North Aral filled "within a year" (World Bank, via Eurasianet); level rose from under 30 m to 38 m quickly,
  42 m by 2008, 12 m above 2003; area 2,550 km2 (2003) -> 3,300 km2 (2008); salinity 30 -> 8 g/L (Wikipedia).
- Fish: 52 t in 2004 (World Bank project record) -> about 8,000 t a year (Times of Central Asia, Global Voices);
  22 species back (Times of Central Asia). 2025 catch reported down from 7.8 to 3.9 thousand t (Global Voices).
- Volume: 18.9 bn m3 (2022) -> 23 bn m3 end 2025 (Ministry via Astana Times, Mar 2026); 23.40 bn m3 on 10 Aug 2026
  (Ministry via Times of Central Asia). Area 3,065 km2 in Feb 2025 (Times of Central Asia).
- Phase 2 (2026-2029, awaiting approval/finance): reconstruct/raise Kokaral, level to 44 m (Baltic), volume 34 bn
  m3, area 3,913 km2; >160 irrigation design estimates in Turkistan and Kyzylorda (Ministry, Astana Times Mar 2026).
  Minister Nurzhan Nurzhigitov: restoring it is "not only an environmental matter but also a social and strategic
  task for Central Asia" (Astana Times).
- Ramsar site since 2012 (Times of Central Asia).

## R5 The south: forests on a seabed, and oil
- Uzbekistan: protective "green cover" on 2 million hectares of the dried bed 2018-2025, at the president's
  initiative (gov.uz, March 2026 release); planting continues in 2026. Kazakhstan: about 1.1 million ha 2021-2025
  (Times of Central Asia).
- Caveat: a 2025 Times of Central Asia report found survival uneven: one site with more than half the trees
  alive, others with almost none (attr). Experts (Pulitzer Center / DW) argue saxaul alone is not enough.
- Uzbekistan is moving toward oil exploration on the drying South Aral bed (Wikipedia) - one line only.

## R6 The new threat: Afghanistan's Qosh Tepa canal
- 285 km planned, from Kaldar (Balkh) to Andkhoy (Faryab); construction began early 2022; phase 1 done Oct 2023;
  phase 2 reported 98 % complete in March 2026 (Afghan National Development Company, attr); completion expected
  late 2026 (Wikipedia). Target: 550,000 ha of new farmland.
- Diversion estimates vary: 6-10 km3 a year is the most cited range, roughly a fifth to a quarter of the Amu Darya's
  average flow (various analysts; attr). The Taliban government says Afghanistan is using its fair share and has
  the right to the water. Afghanistan was not part of the 1992 Central Asian water agreements (do not say; not
  verified) -> omit.
- Dec 2023: Sentinel-2 showed water spreading about 9 km along the canal's north bank; officials denied a breach.

## R8 Added 2026-11-08 (second research pass, for length)
- Northern river reversal: approved by the 25th CPSU Congress in March 1976; cancelled by a resolution of
  14 August 1986 over cost and opposition (Siberian communities, scientists); hardly any construction done. One
  version would have sent up to 40 km3 a year south toward the Aral (attr). Russian officials deny plans to revive
  it; some Central Asian officials still hope (Wikipedia "Northern river reversal"; Fergana).
- Muynak cannery: kept running in Soviet times on frozen fish brought from the Baltic and North Atlantic, "more
  than 2,000 miles by rail and truck" (= more than 3,200 km); after 1991 the Baltic states stopped the shipments;
  4 million cans in 1993 of a 27 million capacity (James Rupert, Alicia Patterson Foundation, 1990s reporting).
- Muskrat trapping in the deltas once yielded up to 500,000 pelts a year (Wikipedia).
- Climate: losing the lake made winters colder and summers hotter (NASA; Micklin 2007).
- Brine shrimp (Artemia) in the Uzbek West Aral: first found 1998, probably brought by migrating birds; first large
  commercial harvest of cysts 2010; harvesters winter in cabins at down to -40 degrees; a biologist warns it may be
  "a matter of years" before the Uzbek side cannot support them (Dialogue Earth).
- North Aral: Ramsar site with the Syr Darya delta since 2012; more than 6 bn m3 flowed in over 2023-2025; farmer
  subsidies for water-saving technology raised from 50 % to 80 % (Times of Central Asia; Astana Times).
