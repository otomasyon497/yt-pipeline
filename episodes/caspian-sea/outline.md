# caspian-sea: outline (target 16-18 min, about 2,350 words)

Working title: "The World's Largest Lake Just Fell Below Its 1977 Record" (to test with yt-package in step 5)
Central question: the Caspian has risen and fallen before. Is this one more swing, or a sea that is leaving for good?
Answer at the end: the science says it will keep falling and the north will empty; nobody agrees yet on how much
of that is ours, and the five countries only started asking together in October 2026.

## 0. Cold open (about 1:10, no card)
- HERO: Sentinel-2 wipe at Kulaly island, 2021 vs 2026: water becomes seabed, new islands, a seal island now joined
  to the mainland.
- January 2026: the level passes the 1977 record low. October 2026: the five presidents meet for the first time on it.
- Open loop: the last time this sea fell this far, the Soviet Union fired nuclear charges to save it, and then the
  sea surprised everyone. Title.

## 1. A Sea With No Exit (about 2:30, curious)
- Map: the five countries; three basins drawn by depth (north 5-6 m on average; south over 1,000 m).
- The water budget: rivers in (the Volga about 80 %), evaporation out, no outlet. Route: the Volga into the delta.
- Why the north matters: under 1 % of the water but the seals' ice, the sturgeon rivers, the oil fields, the ports.
  A small drop moves its shore by kilometres.
- Bridge: the sea has done this before, and the last time it scared a superpower.

## 2. The Soviet Panic (about 2:30, tension)
- 1929-1977: about 3 m down (level chart).
- 1971: three nuclear charges for a canal to pour northern rivers into the Volga (S2 view of the crater lake).
- 1980: dam across the Kara-Bogaz-Gol strait; the bay is dry by 1984 and salt blows across the steppe (ISS/MODIS
  photos, S2 view of the strait with the dam remains).
- 1978-1995: the sea turns and climbs about 2.5 m, floods 400,000 ha of coast; the dam is opened in 1992; the river
  plan is dead by 1986.
- ASK 1: "The Caspian fooled the experts once. Is today's drop another swing, or something new?"

## 3. The Fall (about 2:30, tension)
- 1996-2026: 2.45 m down (Kazakh sector); the rate rose from about 8 cm a year to about 23 in 2020-2024 (verify).
- OWN ANALYSIS: MODIS wipe of the north-east, Sept 2000 vs Aug 2025; our pixel count: about 15,700 km2 of water gone
  in this box (the Kazakh institute: 16,384). Kaydak bay: green water to white salt (plus the ISS photo).
- Whole sea: 28,644 km2 since 1996, an Albania. OWN CALC: about 900 km3 of water, 12 GERD reservoirs (link to the
  first video).
- Bridge: the water is gone. Where it went is where the arguments start.

## 4. Who Is Draining It? (about 2:30, curious)
- Side A, the sky: warmer air evaporates more (Chen et al. 2017: evaporation the dominant driver, about half).
- Side B, us: UNU-INWEH 2026: evaporation explains only about 40 %, the rest is human use; up to 7 m more in some
  scenarios. Map: the Volga basin and its reservoir chain; 80 % of the inflow passes one country.
- The countries: Russia points at the climate, voices in Azerbaijan at the dams, a Kazakh official calls the Volga
  "the easiest explanation" (verify all three).
- ASK 2 (middle): "Warmer skies or thirsty dams: what do you think is draining the Caspian?"
- One content-tied subscribe line.

## 5. What a Smaller Sea Costs (about 2:30, tension)
- Ports: Aktau and Kuryk dredging (USD 40 and 57 million), on the China-Europe Middle Corridor.
- Oil: Kashagan and Filanovsky landlocked at 5-15 m (Oil Rocks photos for the Soviet oil past).
- Seals: from a million to about 100,000; breeding ice -81 % at 5 m; Durnev island joined the mainland (S2).
- Iran: Gorgan Bay drying, the dredged channel (S2 wipe).
- Bridge: and all of this is at today's level. The forecasts go much lower.

## 6. The Shore of 2100 (about 2:00, tension -> calm)
- Chart: about 8 m (SSP2-4.5) and about 14 m (SSP5-8.5) by 2100; 5-10 m even below 2 C.
- OWN MAP: the shoreline at -5, -10 and -14 m from NOAA's seafloor map: at -10 m the northern basin is almost gone.
  Ports and oil fields marked on the new map (Turkmenbashi 16 km inland, Lagan 115 km).
- ASK 3: "Dredge and adapt to a smaller sea, or spend billions trying to refill it?"

## 7. Five Countries, One Commission (about 1:30, calm)
- Avaza, 9 October 2026: the commission to find the causes, first meeting 2027; action plan 2026-2036.
- Close on Kulaly (wipe callback): new islands keep appearing; the arguments have only just begun.
- ASK 4 / pinned comment: "Whose problem is the Caspian: the five countries on its shore, or the whole warming world?"
