## Thesis (tested so far, holds)
The Caspian has always risen and fallen (about -3 m 1929-1977, about +2.5 m 1978-1995), but the fall since 1996
passed the 1977 low in January 2026 and sped up in the 2020s. Peer-reviewed projections put the level 5-21 m lower
by 2100 (5-10 m even if warming stays below 2 C), which would empty almost the whole northern basin. Why it is
falling is disputed (warmer air evaporating more vs. people using the Volga's water), and in October 2026 the five
shore states set up a commission to find out. [SP23] [C25] [UNU26] [CH17] [SUM26]

## The sea
- World's largest lake / landlocked water body. Area about 371,000 km2 without Garabogazkol (386,400 km2 gross) [CS];
  "about 387,000 km2" [C25]. Volume 78,200 km3; deepest point 1,025 m. [CS]
- Five shore states: Kazakhstan, Russia, Azerbaijan, Iran, Turkmenistan. More than 15 million people live on its coasts. [CS] [C25]
- Three basins: north, average depth 5-6 m, under 1 % of the water; middle, average 190 m, 33 %; south, over 1,000 m, 66 %. [CS]
- Surface about 28-29 m below the ocean; salinity about 1.2 %, a third of sea water. [CS]
- No outlet: rivers bring water in, evaporation takes it out. The Volga gives about 80 % of the inflow. [CS] [EN26] [UNU26]
- The Volga is the sea's only link to the world ocean (via canals). [C25]

## Two centuries of swings
- 1929-1977: fell about 3 m, to about -29.0 m (Baltic datum) in 1977. [HIST] [CS]
- The Soviet response:
  - 23 March 1971, "Taiga": three 15-kiloton nuclear charges fired together in Perm region to test digging the
    Pechora-Kama canal, which would have sent northern river water into the Kama, the Volga and on to the Caspian.
    The crater is over 600 m long (about 700 x 350 m) and is now a lake. The nuclear digging was dropped; the whole
    northern-rivers reversal was abandoned in 1986. [PKC] Location 61.306 N, 56.599 E.
  - March 1980: the strait to Kara-Bogaz-Gol (Garabogazkol, about 18,000 km2), seen as an evaporation pan stealing
    water, was dammed. The bay dried out completely by 1984 (one source: autumn 1983); blowing salt harmed soils and
    health downwind. [KBG]
- 1978-1995: the sea turned and rose about 2.5 m, to -26.62 m in 1995. [KZH] [CH17: +12.74 cm/yr 1979-1995]
  More than 400,000 ha of coast flooded; roads, power lines and fish farms were destroyed and people resettled [RISE].
  Damage estimates vary from 2 to 15 billion dollars: do not quote a number.
- 1992: Turkmenistan's president Niyazov had the dam opened; the bay refilled. [KBG] Remains of the dam are still
  visible. [KBG] A road bridge across the strait opened on 2 November 2025 (354 m). [KBG]

## The fall since 1996
- Kazakh sector: -26.82 m (1996) -> -29.27 m (2025), 2.45 m. [EN26] Kulaly island station, July 2026: -29.42 m. [EN26]
- 9 January 2026: -29.11 m, below the 1977 record low. [RA26] Russian researchers: -29.4 m in 2025, below 1977. [SHI-iz]
- "Lowest in about 200 years" (Iranian environment official, Aug 2026), falling 10-20 cm a year, about 2 m since the
  mid-1990s. [IN26] Other outlets say 48 or 400 years: the script says "below its 1977 record".
- Speed: 2005-2019 about 8 cm/yr; 2020-2024 about 23 cm/yr (Shirshov Institute). [SHI] (verify)
- Area: 28,644 km2 lost 1996-2025, 7.4 % of the 1996 sea, about the size of Albania (28,748 km2). [SHI]
  UNU-INWEH: about 24,000 km2 since the mid-1990s. [UNU26] Kazakh institute, north-eastern study area: about
  16,384 km2 of water lost 2000-2025. [EN26]
- New land: Durnev island, where thousands of seals gathered in 2021, could be reached by car by 2023 and later
  joined the mainland. New unnamed islands keep appearing near Kulaly island; some are about 1 km long and
  200-300 m wide (aerial survey, autumn 2026). [EN26]

## Why it is falling (contested: give both sides)
- Climate: Chen et al. 2017 found that more evaporation over the sea plays the dominant role; press summary: about
  half of the 1996-2015 fall from evaporation, the other half from rain and river changes. [CH17]
- Human use: UNU-INWEH (2026) says evaporation explains only about 40 % of the loss since the mid-1990s and the
  rest "points overwhelmingly toward human activity"; rainfall over the Volga basin rose slightly. [UNU26]
  A University of Alabama researcher: human extraction could add up to 7 m of decline in some scenarios. [EN26]
- National positions (verify): Russia links it mainly to climate; Azerbaijani voices point to the Volga dams; a
  Kazakh official said blaming the Volga is "the easiest explanation" but not the whole story. [DEBATE]

## What a smaller sea costs
- Ports: Kazakhstan is dredging Aktau (about USD 39.8 million, depths of 6-7 m, due end-2026) and Kuryk (about
  USD 56.8 million). Azerbaijan dredges Alat and Baku areas. [PORTS] These ports carry the "Middle Corridor" between
  China and Europe. (verify cargo figures before using any)
- Oil: Kashagan (Kazakhstan) and Filanovsky (Russia) rely on ships; a 5-15 m fall would leave them landlocked. [C25]
- In a 10 m scenario (verify the scenario per port): the shore moves 1 km or more from Baku, Anzali and Aktau, 16 km
  from Turkmenbashi and 115 km from the planned port of Lagan. [C25]
- Seals: Caspian seals, found nowhere else, fell from over a million a century ago to under 10 % of that [CS]
  (about 100,000 [EN26]); IUCN Endangered since 2008. Pups are born on the northern ice, mid-January to early March.
  [SEAL] A 5 m fall could cut breeding ice by up to 81 % and cut off every haul-out site [C25]; island haul-out area
  already shrank more than 15-fold since 1995 [EN26].
- Sturgeon: six species [CS]; shallow summer-autumn habitat could shrink 25-45 %, cutting access to spawning rivers. [C25]
- Iran: Gorgan Bay is drying; a channel was dredged in 2022 and when it opened in 2023 the bay rose 40 cm [GOR];
  late-2025 warnings gave the bay a few years. [GOR]
- Dust from the exposed seabed may carry salt and industrial pollution. [C25]

## The future
- Samant & Prange 2023 (15 CMIP6 models): about 8 m (models 2-15 m) by 2100 under SSP2-4.5, about 14 m (11-21 m)
  under SSP5-8.5; "complete desiccation of the northern Caspian basin". [SP23]
- Court et al. 2025: 5-10 m likely by 2100 even below 2 C of warming; up to 21 m with more. In a 10 m scenario about
  112,000 km2 dries; four of ten Caspian ecosystem types vanish; protected-area cover falls by up to 94 %. [C25]
- Prange et al. 2020: 9-18 m this century; at 18 m about 143,000 km2 (37 %) becomes land. [P20 via CS]
- Koriche (Univ. of Alabama): up to 8 m by 2100 in an intermediate scenario; a 5 m fall would expose about
  77,000 km2 of the northern seabed. [EN26]
- Scientists still disagree on timing; the 1978 turnaround is the reminder that the sea can surprise. [CH17] [KZH]

## The response
- 9 October 2026, Avaza (Turkmenistan): first Caspian Environmental Summit of the five presidents. They signed a
  decision creating a commission (deputy heads of government of Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Turkmenistan; high-ranking
  representatives of Iran and Russia) whose first job is to identify the causes of the fall; first meeting in
  Azerbaijan in the first half of 2027; a Caspian Sea Level Fluctuation Action Plan 2026-2036 was presented. [SUM26]
- Kazakhstan says it has directed about 6.16 km3 of river water into the sea in 2026 (verify). 2018 Aktau convention
  on the sea's legal status (verify date and wording).

## Own calculations (show on screen as our analysis)
1. NE Caspian from orbit, MODIS Terra, same box (44.4-47.7 N, 49.6-54.6 E), water = dark in bands 7-2-1:
   5 Sep 2000: about 60,400 km2 of open water; 24 Aug 2025: about 44,700 km2. About 15,700 km2 turned to land,
   close to the Kazakh institute's 16,384 km2 [EN26]. Caveat: one day per year; 13 Sep 2001 gave 55,600 km2 (wind
   and murky water move the shallow edge), so the final figure uses the median of several clear days per year.
   FINAL (used in the script): every clear Aug-Sep day (cloud < 1 %, no swath gap), 0.008-degree grid:
   2000-2002: 17 days, median 57,982 km2 (54,900-60,860); 2023-2025: 13 days, median 44,606 km2 (41,881-47,855).
   Loss about 13,400 km2 (23 %). Script: "about fifty-eight thousand", "about forty-five thousand", "about thirteen
   thousand"; the Kazakh institute's 16,384 km2 is quoted next to it as its own study area and period, not as a match.

   km2 x 2.45 m = about 913 km3. That is about 12 times the GERD reservoir (74 km3, our first video) [GERD]. Also
   about 3.6 years of the Volga's flow if the Volga carries about 254 km3/yr (verify the Volga figure).

   - -5 m: 64,200 km2 falls dry (18 %); northern basin left: 9,900 km2 (84 % gone)
   - -8 m: 76,100 km2 (22 %); north left: 6,100 km2 (90 % gone)
   - -10 m: 85,400 km2 (24 %); north left: 3,900 km2 (94 % gone). Script: "less than four thousand are left"
   - -14 m: 101,800 km2 (29 %); north left: 900 km2 (98 % gone). Script: "almost nothing remains"
