The Florida Keys hang on a single road. It runs a hundred and thirteen miles out to sea. It crosses forty-two bridges, and about eighty thousand people live along it. Their food comes down it, their power, even their drinking water. But most of it sits only a few feet above the sea. So why would anyone build a highway across the sea, and can Florida keep it there?

In 1935, the sea rose over the Florida Keys and washed away forty miles of railroad. Florida did not give the islands up. It built a highway on what was left, a single road a hundred and thirteen miles long. But that road now carries everything the Keys have, and the sea is still rising. So why hang a whole chain of islands on one road?

Every drop of water drunk in Key West has travelled about a hundred and thirty miles down a pipe. The pipe runs beside a single road across the Florida Keys. But that road is the only way in, and the only way out. So why would anyone put a whole chain of islands on one road across the sea?

In April 1982, the Border Patrol put a roadblock on one road in southern Florida. Within a week, Key West declared that it had left the United States. It sounds like a joke, but it shows how the Florida Keys work. One road is the only door. So why would anyone build a highway across the sea?

Why does a chain of islands in Florida, home to eighty thousand people, have just one way in and one way out? The road to Key West is bigger than the islands it joins. But it sits a few feet above the sea, and the sea is coming up. So why did America build a highway across the sea?
