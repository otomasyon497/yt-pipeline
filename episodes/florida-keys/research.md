## Facts (round 1)
- R1 Overseas Railroad: work began 1905; first train into Key West 22 Jan 1912 with Flagler (82) aboard; Homestead to
  Key West 128 mi (1912 booklet, 17.2 mi on bridges) [CHECK primary]; hurricanes 1906 (quarter boat no. 4, ~100 dead),
  1909, 1910; Lake Surprise fill took ~15 months; Pigeon Key camp ~400 men [CHECK]. Cost: no primary figure yet (">$20 M"
  is weak): do not use a number.
- R2 Why Key West: deep harbour on the Straits of Florida; wrecking court; county seat; Navy base; Flagler's bet on the
  Panama Canal trade (opened 1914) [CHECK source]. Key West the largest city in Florida in the 1880 and 1890 censuses
  [CHECK Census 1890].
- R3 1928 highway with a ferry gap: Lower Matecumbe to No Name Key, ~40 mi, ~4 h, twice a day (Geography Docs; CHECK
  Hopkins, Tequesta 1986).
- R4 1935 Labor Day hurricane: landfall 2 Sep near Long Key, 892 mb (lowest US landfall), 185 mph 1-min winds (Landsea
  et al. 2014), surge 18-20 ft; WWI veterans in FERA road camps; rescue train at Islamorada, all but locomotive 447
  overturned; deaths: sources differ (NWS 408, FERA 423 / 485, VA 488): "more than 400". About 40 mi of track lost; FEC
  did not rebuild; state bought the right-of-way for $640,000 [CHECK primary].
- R5 Overseas Highway opened 29 Mar 1938 on the railbed; Bahia Honda: road on top of the steel truss, 65 ft up
  (Geography Docs) [CHECK FDOT/NRHP]; tolls until 15 Apr 1954; NRHP ref 79000684 (1979). Length 113 mi vs 106.5 mi:
  [CHECK FDOT].
- R6 New bridges: 37 of 44 bridges replaced in the early 1980s (Florida Memory); new Seven Mile Bridge opened May 1982,
  $42-45 M; old bridge reopened as a trail (2022). Replacement of the current Seven Mile Bridge: FDOT estimate ~$677 M,
  slated ~2030; sufficiency rating 49.1 (Miami Herald, WUSF 2024) [CHECK FDOT].
- R7 The pipe: no usable fresh water in the porous upper-Keys rock (cisterns, rainwater, water shipped by train/barge
  before 1942). Florida Keys Aqueduct Commission created 1937; Navy bought 353 acres at Florida City 18 Aug 1941; 18-in
  pipeline ~130 mi; first water reached Key West 22 Sep 1942 (keyshistory.org, Keys Weekly; Time) [CHECK FKAA history
  page]. Today FKAA: wellfield at Florida City, 1,086 mi of pipe, 45 bridges [CHECK]; reverse-osmosis backup plants.
- R8 The roadblock: Border Patrol checkpoint on US 1 just south of Florida City, April 1982 (Mariel boatlift aftermath);
  federal court refused an injunction; 23 Apr 1982 Mayor Dennis Wardlow declared the "Conch Republic"; the roadblock was
  removed (Florida Historical Society; Wikipedia) [CHECK dates].
- R9 Evacuation: state 24-hour clearance-time model for permanent residents sets the Keys' "carrying capacity" and thus
  the building-permit cap (rate of growth ordinance); county asked in Dec 2024 to lengthen it; 2025 bills 24.5 / 26 h
  with up to 500 permits [CHECK outcome, F.S. 380.0552]; county EM: 36-48 h to move everyone (WLRN 2017).
- R10 Irma 2017: Cat 4 landfall at Cudjoe Key 10 Sep 2017 09:10 EDT, 115 kt, 931 mb (NHC TCR AL112017); 1,179 homes
  destroyed, 2,977 major damage (Monroe County); mobile homes worst (666 destroyed) [CHECK]. Tourists ordered out first,
  then residents.
- R11 Sea level: Key West gauge 8724580, 1913-2024, +2.64 +/- 0.15 mm/yr, 0.86 ft per century (NOAA). Own measurement M1
  from the monthly data (also the last 30 years).
- R12 Roads: raising ~155 mi of county roads ~$1.8 B (Monroe County 2021, Miami Herald); Twin Lakes (Key Largo) $21.97 M
  for 4,633 ft, to 13 in NAVD (county slides) -> ~$25 M a mile; residents' assessments proposed [CHECK].
- R13 People: Monroe County 82,874 (2020), 80,406 (2025 est.); Key West 26,444 (2020) [CHECK Census QuickFacts].
- R14 Reef: 2023 marine heatwave; 97.8-100 % of remaining elkhorn and staghorn died; "functionally extinct" in Florida
  (2025 paper) [CHECK, Science 2025?]. Only a side note for us.

## Gate-1 outline (~2,750 words, ~17.3 min)
- Hook (~230): 113 miles, 42 bridges, one road; everything rides on it: the people, the water pipe, the way out.
  "But" the road sits a few feet above the sea, and the last time the sea came over the islands it took the railroad
  that came first. Teasers: a pipe that carries every drop of fresh water; the day a roadblock made Key West "secede";
  the price of keeping the road out of the water. Question: why would anyone build a highway across the sea, and can
  it be kept? "The first reason is at the end of the road."
- 1. Key West was worth the trip (~350): the harbour, the largest city in Florida in 1890, the Navy, Flagler's Panama
  bet. Short: rivals did the wrecking story.
- 2. A railroad first (~400): 1905-1912, Lake Surprise, the 1906 quarter boat, Long Key viaduct, Seven Mile, 22 Jan 1912.
  The road came later, with a 40-mile ferry gap (1928).
- 3. The sea took the railroad, the road took its place (~450): 1935, the veterans, the rescue train, $640,000, the
  highway on the railbed 1938, Bahia Honda's road on the roof.
- 4. The islands have no water of their own (~400): porous reef, cisterns, the 1942 Navy pipe, M3 + FKAA today; one pipe
  beside one road.
- 5. One road is one door (~450, re-hook): the 1982 roadblock and the Conch Republic; the 24-hour evacuation rule that
  caps houses; Irma 2017 as the test.
- 6. The sea is coming up the road (~350): M1, M2, $1.8 B roads, Twin Lakes, the Seven Mile replacement.
- Ending (~120): pays off the teasers; question to the viewer: "Should Florida keep paying to hold a road above the
  sea, or let the islands go back to boats?" (pinned comment, two-sided).

Cover idea: Sentinel turquoise shallows with the Seven Mile Bridge, words "ONE ROAD" / "113 MILES OF SEA".

## Round 2 (2026-11-27)
- M1 DONE (geo/measure_sealevel.py -> measure_sealevel.txt, NOAA CO-OPS monthly means, 1,345 months 1913-2025):

  2016-2025 is 11.6 in above 1913-1922. Narration: "about a foot higher than when the gauge was installed in 1913, and

- R2 checked: Key West 18,080 in the 1890 census, the largest city in Florida (Jacksonville 17,201); 17,114 in 1900
  (Census twps0076 FL table; USF FCIT). Flagler's case: Key West the closest deep-water US port to the Panama Canal
  (announced 1905, opened 1914) and to Cuba; FEC car ferries Key West-Havana from 1915; freight stayed disappointing
  (Wikipedia Overseas Railroad; KWAHS FEC timeline).
- R7 checked: before 1942 rainwater cisterns (tDAR "No fresh water except that furnished by the rains"); Aqueduct
  Commission act signed 11 Jun 1937; Navy paid about two-thirds; pipeline along the old railroad right-of-way, 128-130 mi
  from the Florida City wellfield; first water at Key West 22 Sep 1942; water took ~6 days to arrive (Wilkinson,
  keyshistory.org). Today 1,086 mi of pipe, 45 bridges, ~17 M gallons a day (Keys Weekly). Use "about 130 miles".
- R9 checked: F.S. 380.0552(9)(a)2 (2025): "hurricane evacuation clearance time for permanent residents of no more than
  24.5 hours" (was 24 h; raised by ch. 2025-190, which also orders new modelling to set permit allocations); mobile home
  residents are not counted as permanent residents (they leave first); Key West included in the study.
- R4 checked: state bought the right-of-way and bridges for $640,000 out of a $3.5 M PWA loan (Wilkinson, keyshistory.org
  osh; Overseas Road and Toll Bridge District, Fla. Sup. Ct. 1936). NWS official estimate 408 dead (commonly cited).
  ~350 of the 718 camp veterans were in Miami for a Labor Day ball game; rescue train held at the Miami River
  drawbridge; at Islamorada a surge of ~17 ft swept the cars off the track; everyone on the train survived
  (myfloridahistory.org / NOAA AOML 80th-anniversary blog). Memorial and crypt 1937.
- R5 checked: Bahia Honda: deepest channel on the line, 24 ft (not 30); a through truss too narrow for cars, so the
  highway deck was built on top of the truss, 65 ft up; replaced 1972, two spans removed for boats (Wikipedia Bahia Honda
  Rail Bridge; NPGallery NRHP). Highway opening: 29 Mar 1938 (Wikipedia) vs a July 1938 celebration: say "in 1938".
- R10 checked: Irma landfall Cudjoe Key 10 Sep 2017; by 12 Sep all 42 bridges inspected and safe; two 300-ft washouts
