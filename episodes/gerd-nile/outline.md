# gerd-nile: outline (target 16-18 min, about 2,350 words)

Working title: "Egypt Called This Dam an Existential Threat. Then It Filled."
Central question: the lake is full; what did it really change, and what decides the next drought?
Answer at the end: the dam did not break Egypt; the missing thing is a rule for the dry years.

## 0. Cold open (about 1:10, no card)
- HERO: Sentinel-2 wipe, March 2020 river valley vs March 2026 lake. "Bigger than Greater London."
- Egypt: "existential threat". Ethiopia: "not a threat, but a shared opportunity".
- Open loop: the people who modelled it say the real test has not come yet. Title.

## 1. A River With Two Halves (about 2:30, curious)
- Draw the White Nile (steady, Sudd loses half) and the Blue Nile (wild summer flood); meet at Khartoum (ISS night).
- Chart: about 85 % of Egypt's Nile water starts in Ethiopia; 53 % passes this one dam site.
- Egypt: 95 % of people within 20 km of the river (Delta at night, ISS); 97 % of its water from outside.
- ASK 1 (~2:00): "Who should have more say: where a river rises, or where it is used?"

## 2. Who Got the Water (about 2:00, curious)
- 1929 veto; 1959 split chart: Egypt 55.5 / Sudan 18.5 / evaporation 10 / Ethiopia 0.
- OWN ANALYSIS chart: the same 55.5 per person, 2,063 m3 (1960) -> 476 m3 (2024), 500 m3 scarcity line.
- Bridge: Egypt learned in the 1980s what happens when the river does not come.

## 3. The Summer the River Almost Stopped (about 2:00, tension)
- Aswan High Dam and Lake Nasser (ISS photos), Abu Simbel moved (1960s photos).
- Lake Nasser 171 m (1980) -> 150.6 m (July 1988); Mubarak's warning; the rain came.
- Lesson: storage is Egypt's insurance. Bridge: now the biggest spare tank is upstream, in another country.

## 4. Ethiopia's Turn (about 2:30, curious)
- April 2011 foundation stone, weeks after Mubarak fell.
- Choropleth: electricity access; about 57 million Ethiopians without power (own calc).
- Paid at home: bonds, salaries; about USD 5 billion. Scale count-ups: 145 m, 1.78 km, 74 km3 (almost 2x Three Gorges), 5,150 MW.
- ASK 2 (middle): "Build without a deal, or wait for one? Which would you have chosen?"

## 5. Ten Years of Not Agreeing (about 2:30, tension)
- 2013 "all options are open"; 2015 Khartoum principles; Washington 35 vs 40 bcm; "blow up that dam" (2020).
- July 2020 first fill: Sudan's water stations (Ethiopia denies). UN 2021. Talks fail Dec 2023. CFA in force 2024.
- Inauguration 9 Sep 2025; Egypt protests to the UN. One content-tied subscribe line.

## 6. The Verdict So Far (about 2:00, calm)
- Chart: five fillings, 540 -> 640 m. CIRA year-by-year frames.
- Researchers: no appreciable harm; wet years + Egyptian planning; both lakes full.
- 2025 Sudan floods: "man-made flood" vs "the dam reduced it"; not settled.
- ASK 3 (strong chapter end): "Did the dam cause the 2025 floods, or soften them?"

## 7. The Test That Hasn't Come (about 2:30, tension -> calm)
- Model: < 7 % yearly chance Egypt must cut, even if Ethiopia tried to hurt it; harming Egypt would cost Ethiopia.
- The danger window: a long drought and the refill after it, both lakes low; Egypt's own cut rules (60/55/50 bcm).
- Egypt's hedges: Bahr el-Baqar, New Delta (NASA before/after).
- Close on the lake (wipe callback): the dam is built; the rules are not.
- ASK 4 / pinned comment: "When the next long drought comes, will the Nile have rules, or a crisis?"
