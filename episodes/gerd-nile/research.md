## Thesis (tested, holds)
The five fillings (2020-2024) did not cause appreciable harm to Egypt, because the filling years were wet and
Egypt planned for it; both reservoirs are now full ("new normal"). Modelling says shortages for Egypt stay
rare even if Ethiopia ran the dam to hurt Egypt, but long droughts, and the refill after them, need
coordination that still does not exist. So the real test is the next multi-year drought, and the missing
piece is a deal, not concrete. [W25] [W20]

## The river
- Length: traditionally about 6,650 km; a 2009 satellite measurement gives 7,088 km. Script: "more than 6,600 km". [NILE]
- Basin: about 2.9 million km2, about 10 % of Africa, 11 countries (Burundi, DR Congo, Egypt, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda). [NILE]
- Average flow at Aswan about 84 bcm/yr (basis of the 1959 deal); 2,757 m3/s ~ 87 km3/yr in [NILE]. Script: "about 84 billion cubic metres".
- White Nile: steady, fed by the Great Lakes; about half of its water evaporates in the Sudd swamps. [NILE]
- Blue Nile: from Lake Tana and the Ethiopian highlands; floods July-October; summer flood about 70 % Blue Nile. [NILE]
- Ethiopian highlands (Blue Nile, Atbara, Sobat) give about 85-86 % of the Nile's water; nearly 90 % at flood peak. [FAO-ETH]
- 53 % of the basin's water originates above the GERD site. [W25 slide 2]
- Blue Nile and White Nile meet at Khartoum. [NILE]
- About 97 % of Egypt's renewable water comes from outside its borders (dependency ratio). [AQUA]
- About 95 % of Egyptians live within 20 km of the Nile and its delta, on about 5 % of the land. [CIA]
- Egypt's per capita water share has fallen below 500 m3 (irrigation minister, 2026). [AHRAM-500]

## Who got the water
- 1929 Anglo-Egyptian agreement: gave Egypt a veto over upstream projects (Britain signing for its East African colonies). [1959]
- 1959 Egypt-Sudan agreement: Egypt 55.5 bcm, Sudan 18.5 bcm, about 10 bcm left for evaporation/losses, of about 84 bcm. Ethiopia was not a party and does not consider itself bound. [1959]
- 2010 Cooperative Framework Agreement signed by upstream states; in force 13 October 2024 after South Sudan's ratification (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Rwanda, Uganda, Burundi, South Sudan). Egypt and Sudan reject it. [CFA]

## Aswan and the 1980s drought
- Aswan High Dam built 1960-1970; Lake Nasser floods more than 480 km of the valley; holds about two years of river flow; 2.1 GW. [NILE]
- Abu Simbel temples were moved in the 1960s UNESCO campaign before the lake rose (Commons photos below).
- Lake Nasser's annual minimum fell from 171 m (1980) to 151 m (1988) [FAO-NASSER]; lowest level ever recorded 150.6 m, July 1988 [BIRD].
- May 1988: President Mubarak warned hydropower at the High Dam could stop completely by July if the fall continued; after heavy rain in East Africa that summer, the minister expected full output again within two months (September 1988). [MEES]

## The dam
- Site: Guba, Benishangul-Gumuz, about 14 km from the Sudan border, on the Blue Nile (Abay); 11.215 N, 35.093 E. [GERD]
- Main dam 145 m high, 1,780 m long, roller-compacted concrete; rock-fill saddle dam about 5 km long, 50 m high. [GERD]
- Reservoir: 74 km3 total (59.2 active), 1,874 km2, up to 246 km long; full supply level 640 m. [GERD]
- Power: 5,150 MW (13 turbines); about 15-15.8 TWh/yr; Africa's largest hydropower plant; expected to more than double Ethiopia's output. [GERD] [W25] [INAUG]
- Cost close to USD 5 billion; financed largely at home through government bonds and contributions (civil servants, diaspora); about USD 1 billion Chinese credit for turbines/electrical works. [GERD]
- Contract 31 March 2011 (Salini Impregilo, now Webuild); foundation stone 2 April 2011 by PM Meles Zenawi (Mubarak had resigned on 11 February 2011). [GERD]
- First power 20 February 2022. Inaugurated 9 September 2025 by PM Abiy Ahmed; Egypt protested to the UN. Abiy: "not a threat, but a shared opportunity". [GERD] [INAUG]
- Ethiopia electricity access 56.6 % (2024), Egypt 100 %; population Ethiopia 132.1 M (2024). [WB]

## Ten years of not agreeing
- 10 June 2013: President Morsi, "all options are open" (after a televised meeting where politicians discussed sabotage). [GERD]
- 23 March 2015: Declaration of Principles in Khartoum (Sisi, Bashir, Ethiopian PM Hailemariam Desalegn): no significant harm, cooperation. [DOP]
- Nov 2019 - Feb 2020: Washington talks (US Treasury, World Bank). Ethiopia offered 35 bcm/yr released and a 5-year fill; Egypt asked 40 bcm/yr and 7 years. Ethiopia did not sign in February 2020. [GERD]
- October 2020: the US president said publicly Egypt might "blow up that dam"; Abiy: Ethiopia "will not cave in to aggression". [GERD]
- 8 July 2021: UN Security Council session. [GERD]
- December 2023: the fourth round in Addis Ababa failed; Egypt blamed Ethiopia's "persistent refusal", said it reserves its right to defend its water security; Ethiopia rejected Egypt's account. [DEC23]
- April 2021 Sisi: "all options are open". [GERD]

## The verdict so far
- "GERD filling did not cause appreciable harm to Egypt because of high annual flows during the filling period and prudent planning efforts by the Egyptian government. Today both the High Aswan Dam reservoir and the GERD reservoir are full: new normal." [W25 slide 14]
- 2020 study: risk of shortage for Egypt during filling relatively low; the new normal benefits Ethiopia and Sudan without significantly affecting Egypt's water users; multi-year droughts need careful coordination. [W20]
- Sudan stands to gain: flood control, more irrigation, less sediment, more hydropower; but uncoordinated releases mean more flooding. [W25 slides 10-11]
- Late Sept - early Oct 2025 floods in Sudan: Egypt called it a "man-made flood" from uncoordinated releases (low releases in late August, then very high in September); Ethiopia said the flooding had "nothing to do with Ethiopia" and that the dam reduced it; Sudan's PM called for a joint detailed review; Sudanese experts cite several causes (GERD releases, climate, weak embankments, a long rainy season). Not settled. [FLOOD25]

## The test that has not come
- Modelling with 100 stochastic flow traces plus the 1980s drought: less than 7 % chance in any year that Egypt would need to cut deliveries, even under an adversarial GERD policy; rare cases (1 in 5 years exceedance) adversarial operation adds 2.72 bcm/yr of shortage vs a compromise policy; extreme cases cut High Dam releases by 8 bcm, briefly, and at a significant cost to Ethiopia. [W25 slides 31-32]
- "Ethiopia would have very little financial justification to inflict harm on Egypt." [W25 slide 33]
- Compromise policy = extra releases during multi-year droughts, and a recovery rule so both reservoirs refill together. [W25 slide 20]
- Egypt's drought rule in the model: High Dam releases cut 5 / 10 / 15 % when its storage falls below 60 / 55 / 50 bcm. [W25 slide 23]
- Negotiating positions: Egypt: existential risk, all water already used by Egypt and Sudan, GERD should support Egypt in drought. Ethiopia: non-consumptive use, right to build, operate for Ethiopian objectives. [W25 slide 13]
- Egypt's hedges: Bahr el-Baqar plant (opened 2021, Guinness "largest wastewater treatment plant", 5.6 million m3/day, irrigates North Sinai) [BAQAR]; "New Delta" desert farming (NASA images 2018 vs 2024).
