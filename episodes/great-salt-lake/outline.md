# great-salt-lake: outline (gate 1)

Target 15-16 min (~2,200 words at 1.08), 8 chapters, written to docs/script-rules.md. Facts: research.md (R1-R12).
Marked "verify": not yet two-sourced; dropped if they stay single.

1. **A Foot From the Bottom** (hook, 0:00-1:30). Sentinel-2 wipe, June 2017 -> October 2026, Farmington Bay and
   Antelope Island (6 ft lower). "On October 10th the lake fell to 4,189.4 feet, less than a foot above its all-time
   low." Scale anchor for US viewers (the lake's area against a state; verify the area at today's level). The turn:
   "Forty years ago, Utah spent $60 million pumping this lake into the desert." The question by 0:30: how does a lake
   go from flooding to failing in one lifetime, and who pays when its bed starts to blow? Stat card: 22 ft below 1986.
2. **The Flood** (1983-1987). Level chart 1960-2026 (R1): 4,211.6 ft in June 1986. Flooded roads and resorts
   (archival photos, licence-checked), the West Desert Pumping Project: April 1987 to June 1989, 26 months, $60 million;
   document shot: USGS WRI 91-4117 page with the pumping lines marked. Re-hook: the pumps still stand, unused.
3. **Where the Water Went.** Map: Bear, Weber, Jordan rivers into the south arm, farm and city icons; Wurtsbaugh et al.
   2017: about 11 ft from water use since the 1850s, agriculture over 60 % (stat card "11 ft"); the 2025-26 winter:
   Utah's snowpack hit a record low on 1 February 2026, snow supplies ~95 % of the state's water (link to the
   colorado-river episode: the same winter). Question card: "Farm water or lake water: who gives first?"
4. **Two Lakes in One.** The 1959 railroad causeway from orbit: pink north arm, blue-green south arm; salinity 317 vs
   142 g/L (bar chart); brine shrimp eggs (35-40 % of the world's; $57 million a year, 2012) and minerals.
5. **The Dust.** About 800 square miles of bed exposed (MODIS Sep 2000 -> Oct 2026 wipe of the whole lake), arsenic in
   the dust; studies (Perry, Grineski, Putman) against the state's PM10 view (both sides on screen); clipping card with a
   short quote. Question card: how much risk is acceptable before paying to keep water in the rivers?
6. **Birds and Snow.** 12 million birds, a third of the world's Wilson's phalaropes, up to 5 million eared grebes
   (map icons); the lake adds 5-8 % of Utah's snowfall; economy $1.3-1.9 billion a year; a dry lake could cost
   $1.7-2.2 billion a year (ECONorthwest 2019). One subscribe line tied to the next look from orbit.
7. **What Would Refill It.** Strategic plan (2024): 4,198-4,205 ft, about 1 million extra acre-feet a year in drought
   (8.6 ft to climb from October's low: own calc); 2026 session: close to $100 million and faster water transfers;
   critics: urban use; a legal 4,198-ft goal rejected. Question card: should Utah set a legal minimum for the lake?
8. **The Winter Ahead** (close). State officials: no record in 2026, but a 2027 record "unavoidable" after another dry
   winter; the November reading; closing question (pinned comment): farms, cities, or the lake: who should give up
   water first?
