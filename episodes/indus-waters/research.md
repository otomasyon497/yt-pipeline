# Indus Waters Treaty: research (2026-11-08)

Episode 8. Frame: the 1960 treaty that split six rivers, survived three wars, and that India put "in abeyance"
in April 2025; a Hague court says it is still in force (31 Aug 2026), India says the court is illegal.
Both governments' statements are positions, attributed as such.

## Sources
- [R1] Wikipedia, "Indus Waters Treaty" (read 2026-11-08): signing 19 Sep 1960 in Karachi by Jawaharlal Nehru,
  Ayub Khan and W. A. B. Iliff (World Bank); effective 1 April 1960; eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India,
  33 MAF mean annual flow; western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan, 135 MAF; India's permitted
  western-river storage: 1.25 MAF general + 1.6 MAF hydropower + 0.75 MAF flood (3.6 MAF); India paid
  GB£62,060,000 over ten years; Indus Basin Development Fund signed the same day by Australia, Canada,
  West Germany, New Zealand, UK, US, IBRD and Pakistan (US$177 million from the US in 1960); 10-year transition;
  Permanent Indus Commission meets at least once a year, "survived three wars"; last (118th) meeting May 2022;
  Lilienthal visit 1951; World Bank proposal 1954 (eastern to India, western to Pakistan); Baglihar cleared by a
  neutral expert; Kishanganga cleared by a Court of Arbitration (Feb 2013); Ratle 850 MW dispute, World Bank
  appointments Oct 2022; India notice to modify Jan 2023; India review notice Sep 2024; Shahpurkandi completed
  2024; after 23 Apr 2025: India flushed Salal and Baglihar off-season without notifying Pakistan.
- [R2] Wikipedia, "India-Pakistan water dispute of 1948": standstill agreement (20 Dec 1947) expired 31 Mar 1948;
  on 1 April 1948 East Punjab shut the water to West Punjab in the main branches of the Upper Bari Doab Canal
  and the Dipalpur Canal (Ferozepur Headworks); Lahore depended on canal water; 5.5 % of Pakistan's cropland
  faced a kharif season without water; restored after about five weeks when Pakistan agreed to an
  Inter-Dominion conference (May 1948); Pakistan later said it signed under duress.
- [R3] PCA press release, The Hague, 31 Aug 2026 (docs.pca-cpa.org, saved as text in the session): Award on the
  Status of the Treaty, unanimous: India's "abeyance" decision could only be a claim of suspension or termination;
  none of the grounds justify it; "the Indus Waters Treaty remains fully in force". Order on interim measures:
  India may not concrete the Ratle dam wall and power intake above certain levels until 90 days after the
  Neutral Expert's final decision, expected July 2027 (work programme: about 16 July 2027). Earlier: Pakistan
  started the arbitration Aug 2016; India asked for a neutral expert Oct 2016; World Bank "paused" Dec 2016,
  lifted 2022; Award on Competence 6 July 2023; Supplemental Award on Competence 27 June 2025 (abeyance cannot
  limit the court's competence); Award on General Interpretation 8 Aug 2025; clarification 8 Nov 2025; Award on
  Maximum Pondage 15 May 2026. Pakistan applied on 4 March 2026; hearing 26-28 April 2026 at the Peace Palace;
  India did not take part. Chair: Prof. Sean D. Murphy (US); five members. India's 23 Apr 2025 statement quoted:
  the treaty "will be held in abeyance with immediate effect, until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abjures its
  support for cross-border terrorism".
- [R4] Dawn / Tribune / PCA summaries of the 8 Aug 2025 award: general rule that India shall "let flow" the
  waters of the western rivers for Pakistan's unrestricted use; exceptions (hydropower) strictly construed;
  design criteria for new run-of-river plants.
- [R5] Al Jazeera, 1 Sep 2026: India rejected the award within hours, calling the court "illegally constituted";
  no enforcement mechanism; Shehbaz Sharif (13 Aug 2026): "every single drop of Pakistan's water is our red line";
  hydrologist Hassan Abbas: India's built storage on the Chenab is roughly 1 million acre-feet, small against
  Pakistan's seasonal needs; India's plants are run-of-river, it "will end up flooding themselves" if it held
  the water; no existential threat "right now".
- [R6] Dawn 1906354 / UN document S/2025/252 (24 Apr 2025): Pakistan's National Security Committee: any attempt
  to stop or divert Pakistan's water "will be considered as an Act of War"; water is a lifeline for 240 million
  people.
- [R7] Dawn 1908705 (May 2025): Chenab at Marala fell from about 35,600 cusecs on Sunday (4 May) to 3,177 cusecs
  on Monday morning (5 May 2025), attributed to the filling of Indian dams; Reuters (via Tribune): Salal and
  Baglihar flushed for three days from 1 May 2025. Figures differ between sources (ARY: 25,382 cusecs on 6 May):
  we say "fell by about nine tenths overnight, according to Pakistani officials quoted by Dawn".
- [R8] Tribune India / Outlook / Deccan Herald, 25 Aug 2025: India warned Pakistan of a "high flood" on the Tawi
  through its High Commission in Islamabad, on humanitarian grounds, not through the Indus commission;
  Pakistan said that bypassed the treaty.
- [R9] Wikipedia, "Kishanganga Hydroelectric Plant": 330 MW; dam near Gurez (34.649 N, 74.751 E); 23.2 km tunnel
  to the Jhelum basin (Bonar Nallah, Wular Lake); inaugurated by Narendra Modi 19 May 2018; the 2013 award
  requires 9 cubic metres a second downstream; Pakistan's Neelum-Jhelum plant (969 MW) downstream.
- [R10] Wikipedia, "Ratle Hydroelectric Plant": 850 MW, Chenab, Kishtwar district (dam ~33.18 N, 75.81 E),
  133 m gravity dam; foundation stone June 2013; Pakistan objects to pondage, intakes, spillway gates.
- [R11] Wikipedia, "Shahpurkandi dam project" + Tribune India / ThePrint: dam on the Ravi (an eastern river,
  India's under the treaty), completed Feb 2024, filling from Nov 2024; built to keep Ravi water that used to
  flow on into Pakistan. Our Sentinel-2 images: river channel Oct-Dec 2018, reservoir 28 Oct 2026.
- [R12] Dawn, 1 Nov 2026 ("India's water blackmail") and ISSI special report (Sep 2026): hydropower capacity
  planned in India's Chenab basin rose from 3,263 MW to 7,620.5 MW in about 18 months; Sawalkot (1,856 MW)
  tendered; Chenab-Beas link tunnel tendered March 2026, reported to divert up to 1.9 MAF a year (Pakistani
  sources; Indian status unconfirmed). Daily Excelsior (June 2026): Ranbir Canal modernisation proposal,
  Rs 998.76 crore, sent to the Central Water Commission.
- [R13] Pahalgam attack 22 Apr 2025, 26 killed (R1, R5, PCA R3 "attack by armed individuals"); India and
  Pakistan fought a four-day conflict 7-10 May 2025 ending in a ceasefire on 10 May (widely reported; R1 refs).
- [R14] Dawn / APP: the Indus basin irrigation system accounts for about 80 % of Pakistan's agricultural
  production (approximate, second-hand; said as "most").
- [R15] Mangla Dam opened 1967; Tarbela completed 1976 (Wikipedia; World Bank archive: both built under the
  Indus Basin Project that the 1960 fund paid for).
- [R16] Natural Earth 10m rivers (public domain) for the six river lines; Copernicus Sentinel-2; NASA MODIS.
