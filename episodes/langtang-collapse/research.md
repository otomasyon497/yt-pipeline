# Langtang collapse (Nepal-Tibet flood, 26 Aug 2026): research (started 2026-11-09)

Episode 10 candidate. Lead from the topic radar (2026-11-09, 75-day wider search): videos on this disaster drew millions
of views on small channels (2.7 M on 8 k subscribers, 4.1 M on 16.6 k, 5.2 M on 35.7 k). Interest is proven; the field is
crowded, so our angle must be our own: the satellite record of one valley hit in 2015, 2025 and 2026, and the
100 km path measured from orbit.
Status: first pass from searches. Most facts below come through Wikipedia and secondary pages. Read the primaries (USGS,
ESA, ICIMOD, Kathmandu Post, Nepal NDRRMA, the studies) before the script. Tolls changed for weeks: cite one dated
official figure, and say it is dated.

## Sources
- [R1] Wikipedia, "2026 Nepal-Tibet floods" (read 2026-11-09 through a page summary, so numbers need the cited primaries):
  - Seismic signal Ms 5.2 at 02:52 UTC (08:37 NPT) just north of Langtang Lirung; USGS first listed an M4.4 earthquake, then
    found the signal came from the collapse. GFZ: Mw 5.7. A second landslide signal three hours later (Ms 4.2).
  - Source: ice and rock from about 5,200-5,400 m; block about 600 m wide (Dan Shugar, University of Calgary); fell about
    1,200 m. Newcastle University: began about 4,800 m, fell over 1,200 m. University of Reading: about 5,200 m, a glacier
    section 1-1.3 km wide. Volume: 197-492 million m3 (an analysis based on USGS/GFZ); BBC Nepali, 8 Oct: a study links it to
    roughly 100 million m3 of glacier ice. CONFLICTING: pick one primary and attribute it.
  - Glacier north of Langtang Lirung moved about 10 mm/month from 8 Jan to 18 Aug 2026, speeding up in the weeks before
    (satellite analysis); Nature, 2 Sep: warning signs visible beforehand; Gizmodo: glacier sped up for weeks.
  - Gyirong Port (Tibet): flood destroyed it seven minutes after the signal (camera footage). The port handled about 30 % of
    Nepal-China trade. Gyirong had built a new flood wall, dikes and held an evacuation drill after 2025; overwhelmed in minutes.
  - Warning: Rasuwa officials told the Department of Hydrology and Meteorology at 09:00 NPT; texts by 09:15, 679,295 messages.
    Kathmandu Post: the alert went out 38 minutes after the collapse, "It wasn't enough". Upstream stations swept away first;
    the system was built for monsoon floods (a Stimson Center fellow: a monitoring "blind spot").
  - Debris about 52 m/s (190 km/h); about 100 km downstream; water up to 80 m above the river at settlements (Prasai 2026).
    Places named in Nepal: Timure, Dhunche, Mailung, Syafrubesi, Betrawati, Trishuli, Devighat (Nuwakot).
    Bodies carried about 250 km, found in Nawalparasi and in Kushinagar and Maharajganj, Uttar Pradesh, India (BBC Live).
  - Two barrier lakes in Tibet; one of 2 million m3 overflowed slightly on 28 Aug and was largely empty by 30 Aug.
  - Toll, Nepal (government, 5 Oct): 1,455 dead, 5,285 missing, 9,287 injured (infobox 1,498+ / 5,804+: conflicting).
    Tibet (16 Sep): 43 dead, 519 missing. Kathmandu Post 12 Sep: 1,386 dead, 5,130 missing. About 650 foreign nationals
    from 38 countries among the missing (1 Oct). Over 900 people at 12 hydropower projects missing, about 500 believed in
    tunnels (other figures differ widely). Search and rescue ended 5 Oct: 13,795 rescued.
  - Damage: 7,570 houses destroyed; NPR 277.79 bn damage (US$1.8 bn), reconstruction NPR 723.67 bn (US$4.78 bn) per the
    Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment; 12 hydropower projects, seven operating plants of 256 MW (Rasuwagadhi, Chilime,
    Trishuli 3A, Trishuli, Devighat ...), 3,795 MW under construction affected; about 430.7 MW disrupted, about 10.6 % of
    Nepal's hydro and solar output; 68 suspension bridges hit, 33 beyond repair; 69 km of road.
  - Human detail: Trishuli headteacher Rajendra Dawadi evacuated about 900 children ten minutes before the flood reached
    the school. Cabinet proposed cutting the wait before a missing person is declared dead from 12 years to 45 days.
  - China removed videos and eyewitness posts from social media (NYT, BBC); at least 10 people penalised for "misleading" posts.
  - Misinformation: early posts blamed Chinese dams; imagery pointed to a collapse on the Nepal side near Langtang Lirung.
- [R2] ESA, "Nepal flash flood imaged by satellites", 31 Aug 2026: Sentinel-2 12 Aug and 24 Aug (before), 27 Aug (after);
  Landsat-9 on 26 Aug about two hours after the collapse; Copernicus EMS activation EMSR927; International Charter activated.
  ESA's own images are CC BY-SA IGO: do not reuse; render our own from Sentinel-2 / Landsat (free data).
- [R3] EarthSky, 27 Aug 2026: 02:52:10 UTC; USGS first an M4.4; Dave Petley: "the seismic signal is almost certainly the rock
  ice avalanche and debris flow, not a tectonic event"; no evidence of a glacial lake outburst.
- [R4] The 2015 precedent: the 25 Apr 2015 earthquake broke a hanging glacier block on Langtang Lirung's south side; debris
  buried almost all of Langtang village within minutes (Himal Southasian). Official toll about 306-310 (Himal; Wikipedia),
  locals say closer to 500; 55 guesthouses; ICIMOD: the eight highest villages of the valley were destroyed.
  NASA Earth Observatory "Landslide in Langtang Valley" (image 85812): NASA imagery, public domain.
- [R5] The 2025 precedent: 8 Jul 2025, an outburst of a supraglacial lake on a glacier in Tibet (Purepu / Purugu, spellings
  differ) flooded the Lende / Bhotekoshi: Friendship (Miteri) Bridge at Rasuwagadhi washed away, Rasuwagadhi 111 MW plant and
  the dry port damaged, about 250 MW shut down; NDRRMA situation report: 11 dead, 18 missing (Kathmandu Post 11 Jul 2025;
  Himal Press). The road was still under repair when 2026 hit (R1).
- [R6] Wider frame: Thame, 16 Aug 2024: rock avalanche above a glacial lake; lakes released 459,000 m3; 25 homes destroyed,
  135 displaced, no deaths (daytime); five significant GLOFs in the Everest region in under 50 years (ICIMOD, Oct 2025).
  Sikkim, 3-4 Oct 2023: South Lhonak lake outburst destroyed the 1,200 MW Teesta III dam at Chungthang; tolls differ
  (55 dead and 74 missing in one study; 92+ on Wikipedia). Chamoli, Feb 2021: rock-ice avalanche about 27 million m3,
  80 % rock, 20 % ice (Shugar et al., Science 2021).
- [R7] ICIMOD HI-WISE report, June 2023: HKH glaciers could lose 30-50 % of volume by 2100 at 1.5-2 C, up to 75 % in the
  eastern Himalaya at 3 C, up to 80 % at 4 C (business as usual); glaciers lost mass 65 % faster in 2011-2020 than the decade
  before. "200 dangerous glacial lakes": one secondary source only, check the report.

- [R10] Volume estimates differ by a factor of ten: HiRISK preliminary 142 million m3 into the valley; a Nepali geologist's
  team 25 million (up to 40); other experts up to 100 million (OnlineKhabar, "ice-mass-bhotekoshi-flood"; AFP via Malay Mail
  4 Sep). Say "tens of millions of cubic metres, estimates differ" or show the range. A Chinese preliminary study
  (Sentinel-2) found the glacier on the north face moving unusually fast in recent years and no rain trigger; the glacier
  retreated about 450 m between 1990 and 2020 (OnlineKhabar, "langtang-lirung-glacier-moving"; antarcticglaciers.org).
  Chamoli 2021 for scale: about 27 million m3 (Shugar et al., Science 2021).
