## Ch. 3 — How a delta disappears

Scale numbers
- USGS SIM 3381, Couvillion, Beck, Schoolmaster & Fischer, released 12 Jul 2017, doi 10.3133/sim3381: net change
  1932-2016 = -4,833 km² observed; modelled -5,197 ± 443 km² (2,006 ± 171 sq mi) ≈ 25 % of the 1932 land area. Loss rates
  from a high of 83.5 km²/yr (32 sq mi/yr) to 28.0 km²/yr (10.8 sq mi/yr) recently: a football field every 34 minutes at
  the peak, every 100 minutes now — V https://www.usgs.gov/news/national-news-release/usgs-louisianas-rate-coastal-wetland-loss-continues-slow
  Couvillion: "The lack of a major hurricane strike since 2008 is probably the main reason we've seen a decrease in the
  rate of land loss," / "The most recent observations are promising, but it is not clear that they will persist," —
  V (same page). NOTE: data end in 2016 — before Laura, Delta, Ida (2020-21). No newer USGS coastwide total found;
  CPRA published "CRMS Data Synthesis – Coastwide Land Change 1985 to 2021" (Sep 2025) — TODO read it for a post-Ida
  number: https://www.lacoast.gov/reports/project/CRMS_Data_Synthesis_Coastwide_Land_Change_1985_to_2021.pdf
  CONFLICT: "every 90 minutes" (InvestigateTV 10 Nov 2026, A), ~1,900 sq mi (CPRA via LSU 2023), 2,300 sq mi (UCS) vs
  USGS 2,006 sq mi / 100 min. Script uses USGS.
- Axios (Dec 2025, A, 403): new USGS/state land-change maps for St. Bernard, Jefferson, Lafourche, Terrebonne,
  Plaquemines to be unveiled March 2026. TODO: did they come out? (would be the freshest number).
- NOAA Office of Coast Survey retired 31 Plaquemines place names in 2013 (40 by early 2014), e.g. Grand Bayou Carrion
  Crow, Fleur Pond, Yellow Cotton Bay, English Bay, Bay Jacquin, Scofield Bay — A (Grist/geogarage 2014); NOAA blog
  "Geographic names disappear from charts, but not from history" (Meredith Westington) — TODO V
  https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/updates/geographic-names-disappear-from-charts/ . Strong map beat: names fade off.

Causes (one per beat)
1. Levees: after the 1927 flood the river was walled in; sediment no longer spreads over the marsh — A (InvestigateTV
   3 Nov 2026). Edmonds et al. 2023, Nature Sustainability (doi 10.1038/s41893-023-01081-0 per URL, TODO confirm),
   Barataria Basin sediment budget: levees ~40 %, subsurface resource extraction (oil/gas) ~40 %, dams ~20 % of land loss
   — V (LSU release 6 Mar 2023) https://www.lsu.edu/mediacenter/news/2023/03/06ored_twilley_bentley_landloss_nature.php
   Edmonds: "What we found was really surprising." Caveat: one basin.
2. Dams: suspended load down ~50 % since the 1950s, >50,000 dams in the basin (Maloney et al. 2018, Marine Geology 400)
   — A. Blum & Roberts (LSU, 2009): pre-dam 400-500 Mt/yr vs ~205 Mt/yr now — A. Delta lobes: river stopped feeding the
   Lafourche delta ~400 years ago (NASA EO 147739, V).
3. Canals: Turner & McClenachan 2018: 76,247 oil/gas wells permitted, 35,163 on land, 27,483 officially abandoned;
   spoil banks long enough to cross coastal Louisiana 79 times east-west; backfilling all canals ~$335 M = 0.67 % of the
   Master Plan — A (via search of PLOS/LSU repository). TODO V the paper (PLOS ONE 2018 "Reversing wetland death from
   35,000 cuts"). Do NOT use "10,000 miles of canals" (not found in a source).
4. Subsidence: Jankowski, Törnqvist & Fernandes 2017, Nature Communications (274 CRMS sites): mean relative sea-level
   rise 12.0 mm/yr (13.2 Mississippi Delta, 9.5 Chenier Plain); shallow subsidence mean 6.8 mm/yr; >= 60 % of
   subsidence in the top 5-10 m; 65 % of Delta wetlands may keep pace, 58 % of Chenier Plain sites do not — V
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5355890 . Nienhuis et al. 2017 GSA Today: coast subsides 9 ± 1 mm/yr on
   average — A.
5. The sea: NOAA tide gauge Grand Isle 8761724: 9.17 ± 0.36 mm/yr, 1947-2024 (≈ 3 ft per century) — A (NOAA table text
   via search; our fetch of the NOAA table failed). TODO V on https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/sltrends/ station page.
   The table's 3.01 is the same trend in ft/century (3.01 ± 0.12 ft), NOT a global rate. Scale beat: "about three feet
   a century at Grand Isle" — the gauge includes the land sinking (relative sea level). If compared with the global
   rate (~3.3 mm/yr satellite era, NASA), cite that separately — TODO pick the source.
6. Hurricanes: Couvillion quote above (rates fell in years without a big hit); Ida 2021 (ch. 6).
- LSU / Nature Communications 10 Apr 2026 (Carol Wilson, Tracy Quirk; doi 10.1038/s41467-026-69091-y per URL): at sites
  with persistent loss ~82 % lose land by edge erosion, ~10 % by drowning; Birdsfoot marshes gain 1-3 cm/yr where
  sediment reaches them; a 2024 study had said up to 75 % of wetlands could drown by 2070 — V (LSU page)
  https://www.lsu.edu/science/news/2026/04/wilson-nat-comms.php  Quirk: "Not all marsh loss is directly due to sea
  level rise," / "Much of the loss we see — both globally and in Louisiana — has been driven by human activity."
- DO NOT USE: NASA EO 147739 line "sediment added nearly 10,000 sq km of land per year" (reads like a summary error).

  waxlake_tm5_1984312_lrg.jpg (3.6 MB), waxlake_oli_2014298_lrg.jpg; GeoTIFFs available. Credit "NASA Earth Observatory
  images by Jesse Allen, using Landsat data from the U.S. Geological Survey."

## Before/after satellite comparisons (own imagery; Landsat / Sentinel-2 are free, public domain / open)
Weather: Louisiana summers (Jun-Sep) are cloudy with afternoon storms and hurricanes; the clearest stretch is
mid-October to November (dry, post-frontal); Dec-Mar fronts bring clear days too but strong north winds push water
out ("blowout" low tides) and winter-brown marsh — compare SAME SEASON (Oct-Nov) and check the Grand Isle tide at
overpass time where possible. Landsat 5 TM from 1984; Landsat 7 SLC-off stripes after May 2003 (avoid 2003-2012 L7);
Sentinel-2 from mid-2015 (10 m).

## OWN MEASUREMENT (Landsat MNDWI, like tools/demak_shoreline.py)
Method: per site box and year, median MNDWI over up to 4 clearest Oct-Nov Landsat 5/8/9 C2 L2 days (QA cloud/shadow
masked); land = median MNDWI <= 0; report hectares and % change. Marsh is wet: test the threshold on a known stable
land patch (e.g. the ridge with houses on Isle de Jean Charles, or Houma) and report "land or marsh above water".
Water level matters: drop days with strong north-wind blowouts or surge; keep the same month window every year.
1. Isle de Jean Charles loss: box (-90.53, 29.36, -90.45, 29.45) ≈ 7.8 km x 10 km; also a tight box on the island ridge

## Two-sided positions
- Diversions: CPRA/Landry/Dove — too expensive (cost doubled since 2016), legal and permit risk, "questionable
  environmental outcomes", would "break our culture" (fisheries); oyster and shrimp industry (Jurisich, Vujnovich) —
  fresh water and silt would kill oyster beds and push shrimp away; vs scientists/advocates (Karst, Moore, Edmonds
  team, Bentley "the right tool for the job", Creppel, Kolker's Neptune Pass evidence) — only the river can build land at
  scale; the need hasn't changed; politics. CPRA board members (Zeringue, Bourgeois) questioned the process for
  Mid-Breton.
- Federal vs state: the Army Corps suspended an MBSD permit (2025) and wants to throttle Neptune Pass (navigation);
  BP/Deepwater Horizon money (federal-state trustee council) runs out within a decade; Supreme Court moved the Chevron
  case to federal court (industry wins the venue); HUD funded the resettlement but the state ran it; HUD civil-rights
  enforcement cuts led the tribe to drop its complaint.
- Tribe vs state/HUD on the resettlement: tribe — a tribal reunification plan turned "top-down relocation", leaders
  excluded, eligibility widened, HOA imposed, homes defective; state/OCD — voluntary, "extensive and ongoing outreach",
  federal rules required opening it to all residents, warranty repairs done, "the state did not force anyone to leave".
- Oil and gas: parishes/AG — companies dredged canals and owe restoration (verdict $744.6 M); industry (LMOGA, Chevron)
  — "misguided lawsuits", wartime federal work, belongs in federal court.
