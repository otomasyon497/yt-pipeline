## 2. Official land-loss total after 2016

- USGS SIM 3381 (1932-2016, released 12 Jul 2017) is STILL the latest USGS coastwide total — no newer USGS coastwide
  number found (searched USGS, data.gov, ScienceBase). V for "no newer total" only as far as search reaches.
- CPRA "CRMS Data Synthesis – Coastwide Land Change 1985 to 2021", Report Version 01, September 2025, Wood, Constantin,
  Sharp & Daigle (CPRA Lafayette; USGS co-workers incl. Brady Couvillion) — V (PDF read)
  https://www.lacoast.gov/reports/project/CRMS_Data_Synthesis_Coastwide_Land_Change_1985_to_2021.pdf
  It does NOT give a coastwide square-mile total. It is site-based (390 CRMS sites, 1-km² boxes; aerial photos 2005,
  2008, 2012, 2015-16, 2018 and 2021 "post Hurricane Ida"). Usable findings (V):
  - 15 % of the network: "Continuous, ongoing land loss in saline marshes on the lower deltaic plain", isolated to
    fringe marshes in Barataria, Terrebonne and Breton Sound; "Loss appears to be due to erosion of otherwise healthy
    vegetation (not drowning in place)."
  - 66 % of the network: landscape stability.
  - "There is an emerging land loss hot spot in the Calcasieu/Sabine Basin in an area that was thought to be
    geologically stable" — loss within CRMS sites there 2005-2021 "around 8% lost", same scale as Barataria and
    Terrebonne.
  - "The only landscape collapse happening now is in the Calcasieu/Sabine Basin. Ongoing deltaic plain land loss
    appears to be due to tidal erosion and hurricanes."
  - Laura and Delta (2020) and Ida (2021) named as the loss events in the variable groups.
  Script use: a post-Ida qualitative line ("monitoring through 2021 shows the loss now concentrated on the eroding
  marsh edges ... and a new hot spot in the southwest"), NOT a new total.
- "March 2026 USGS/state parish maps" (Axios 3 Dec 2025): NOT FOUND — no release located; Axios page 403. Only a
  NOAA shoreline dataset "Port of Plaquemines LA2213-CS-T" updated 4 Mar 2026 (shoreline product, not a land-loss
  map) — A https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/73449 . Do not mention the maps.
- Other post-2016 figures seen (A, do not use as official): Grist/Verite 3 Nov 2026 "roughly 11 square miles per year";
  Times Free Press 8 Sep 2026: Plaquemines lost "more than 250 square miles ... over the past 60 years".
