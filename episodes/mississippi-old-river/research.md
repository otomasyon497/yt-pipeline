# Why the Mississippi Wants to Leave New Orleans (research, started 2026-11-20)

Demand (topic_radar --check, 2026-11-20): PASS, 5 videos at 3x their channel in 365 days (Geo Crumbs 99.6x,
Section Line 45x, Quietly Enormous 39x, Terrain Logbook 31x, Waterline Atlas 21x). Competitors: competitors.md.
Angle: the river has already moved many times (old deltas and bayous on the map), the capture is visible today (the
only growing land in Louisiana is at the Atchafalaya mouth), people sped it up (Shreve's cut, the raft), one structure
holds it, and 2023 showed what the old channel turns into without the river's push (salt water).

## The river moves (the delta cycle)

| Fact | Value | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| River below Baton Rouge began ~5,000-6,000 years ago as sea level neared today's | "migrated back and forth across Louisiana at least seven times, each time forming a delta" | USACE brochure 2009, p. 3 | OK |
| Number of delta lobes | 16 lobes in the past ~6,000 years, grouped in complexes (Teche, St Bernard, Lafourche, Plaquemines-Modern); >100 radiocarbon dates | Frazier 1967 (GCAGS abstract), via USGS/LSU summaries | OK (abstract) |
| Arc of movement | left alone, the lower river "would probably swing across a 200-mile arc every few thousand years" | NASA Earth Observatory, "Taming the Mississippi River", 18 Mar 2015 | OK |
| Mechanism | river lengthens its delta, slope falls, bed rises above surrounding land, a flood breaks out to a shorter, steeper route (avulsion) | Fisk 1952 sect. 216; NOAA; Corps brochure | OK |
| Bayou Lafourche | the Mississippi's bed roughly 800-2,500 years ago | historical marker (hmdb 111250); NOAA | CHECK (ages approximate; say "about a thousand years ago") |
| Bayou Teche | the main course ~2,800-4,500 (or 3,800-5,500) years ago | Wikipedia; marker; sources disagree | CHECK: say "thousands of years ago" |
| Lobe ages (Wikipedia, citing Coleman) | Maringouin ~7,500-5,500; Teche 5,500-3,500; St Bernard 4,000-2,000; Lafourche 2,500-500; Plaquemines-Balize last ~1,500 yrs | Wikipedia "Mississippi River Delta"; NSF paper (Yao et al. 2020, Geomorphology) gives a younger set | CHECK: do not put per-lobe ages on screen |

## The shortcut next door

| Fact | Value | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old River location | ~50 miles NW of Baton Rouge; connects Red, Atchafalaya, Mississippi | USACE brochure | OK |
| Route lengths | Atchafalaya 142 miles to the Gulf vs 315 via the Mississippi | USACE brochure p. 6 | OK |
| Fisk's version | "over 300 miles" vs "slightly more than 100 miles" to sea level | Fisk 1952 sect. 3 | OK (use the Corps numbers) |
| 15th century | a westward loop (later Turnbull's Bend) broke into the Red River basin and captured the Red; the Atchafalaya was a small Red distributary | USACE brochure p. 4 | OK |
| 1831 | Capt. Henry M. Shreve (founder of Shreveport) dug a cut across the neck of Turnbull's Bend; the river took the shortcut; the lower part of the abandoned loop stayed open = "Old River" | USACE brochure p. 4; Fisk sect. 33 | OK |
| The raft | a log jam at the head of the Atchafalaya, "perhaps 20 miles" (Fisk) / "30-mile-long" (Corps); began forming ~1778 (Darby) | Fisk sect. 32; USACE brochure p. 5 | OK (say "a log jam some 20 to 30 miles long") |
| 1839 | residents set fire to the raft in a drought (river so low people crossed on a 15-ft plank); fire burned ~20 miles of it to the waterline | Fisk sect. 33, quoting the Atchafalaya River Study | OK |
| 1840-1855 | the State cleared it with snagboats (navigable April 1842); remnants all removed before 1855; "rapid enlargement commenced" (MRC 1881) | Fisk sect. 33-34 | OK |
| Share of flow | Atchafalaya took 5 % of the Mississippi's flow in 1900 and 25 % by ~1950; 15 % -> 30 % of the latitude flow (Mississippi + Red) | Fisk sect. 49 | OK |
| Longer view | <10 % in 1850 -> ~30 % in 1950 | NASA EO 2015 | OK |
| Fisk's forecast | critical stage (~40 % diverted) "between the years 1965-75"; after it, "closure of the Mississippi River below Old River will be rapid and diversion will be uncontrollable" | Fisk 1952 sect. 216-217 | OK (short quote ok, from the report) |
| The 1990 date | the Corps concluded in 1953 that the river would change course by 1990 if unchecked | NASA EO 2015; Loyola LUCEC | OK (NASA) - say "by about 1990" |
| 1953 | MRC report recommends controlling the diversion with structures at Old River | USACE brochure p. 7 | OK |

## What leaving would do (the Corps' own list)

| Fact | Value | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Effects | present channel would become "a saltwater estuary"; refineries, petrochemical plants, grain elevators, power plants depend on fresh water; cities below Baton Rouge incl. New Orleans "hard-pressed to find drinking water"; Atchafalaya Basin could not take the flow without massive flooding | USACE brochure p. 6-7 | OK |
| Bed below sea level | the riverbed between Natchez and the Gulf lies below sea level, so low flow lets denser salt water creep upriver along the bottom | USACE news release, 16 Sep 2024 (DVIDS 480955) | OK |
| Port of South Louisiana | #1 US port by tonnage: 217.5 million short tons (2023); 54-mile stretch between New Orleans and Baton Rouge | USACE Waterborne Commerce table 2023 (via search); port | OK (USACE table) |
| New Orleans water | city water comes from the river; S&WB has 4 intakes | NOLA Ready; Louisiana Illuminator 2 Nov 2023 | OK |
