## Gate-1 outline

Hook (~200 w): a quarter of the country lies below the sea (26 %, PBL); lowest point 6.76 m below NAP near
Nieuwerkerk aan den IJssel; Europe's big airport on the bed of a lake. But the sea did not rise over the Dutch: they
lowered their own land, about 1.9 m on average across the coastal plain (Erkens 2016). Teasers: a lake that ate
villages, a night in 1953, a province younger than most of its people. Question: how does a country sink itself
below the sea it fears, and why do 18 million people stay? First reason.

1. The ground was peat (~550 w). Coastal peat bog above sea level around AD 1000; 1000-1300 farmers drained it
   for fields, the first water boards; drained peat oxidises and compacts, so the land sinks, so they dig deeper
   ditches; 19.8 km3 of peat lost, 66 % drainage, 34 % dug and burned as fuel (turf).
2. Holes full of water (~550 w). Peat digging and storms turned bogs into lakes that grew and ate land
   (Haarlemmermeer); windmills: Beemster 1607-1612, 43 mills, 38 km dyke, Leeghwater, Amsterdam merchants;
   Kinderdijk 1738/1740, UNESCO 1997; Haarlemmermeer drained 1849-1852 by three steam stations (Cruquius);
   Schiphol now on the lake bed below sea level.
3. The sea comes back (~550 w). St Elizabeth flood 1421 (Grote Hollandse Waard lost); 1916 flood ->
   Zuiderzee Works under Cornelis Lely (1919-1986), Afsluitdijk 32 km (1932), new land: Flevoland, Lelystad, Almere,
   a province since 1986.
4. The night of 1953 (~500 w). 31 Jan-1 Feb 1953, spring tide + NW storm; 1,836 dead in the Netherlands,
   ~200,000 ha flooded, 72,000 evacuated; Delta Committee 20 days later; Delta Works: Oosterscheldekering 1986,
   Maeslantkering 1997.
5. Why they stay, and what comes next (~450 w). 59 % of the land is flood-prone; Randstad and Rotterdam port are
   behind the dikes; the peat still sinks (present rates 2-25 mm/yr, Erkens); sea level rise; Room for the River;
   Delta Programme. Closing question for the comments.

## Facts read (with sources)

- PBL (Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency), correction of the IPCC AR4 wording, 2010: "26 per cent of the
  country is below sea level"; "29 per cent is susceptible to river flooding"; 59 % of the land surface (without open
  water and the Wadden Sea) is susceptible to flooding (55 % inside the dyke rings + 4 % outside). IPCC 2007 had
  said 55 % below sea level. https://www.pbl.nl/en/correction-wording-flood-risks-for-the-netherlands-in-ipcc-report
- Erkens, Van der Meulen, Middelkoop 2016, "Double trouble: subsidence and CO2 respiration due to 1,000 years of
  Dutch coastal peatlands cultivation", Hydrogeology Journal, doi 10.1007/s10040-016-1380-4. Read in the PDF:
  "the peat volume loss was 19.8 km3, which lowered the Dutch coastal plain by 1.9 m on average, bringing most of it
  below sea level. At least 66 % of the volume reduction is the result of drainage, and 34 % was caused by the
  excavation and subsequent combustion of peat." And: "between AD 1000 and 1300, the coastal peatlands were drained
  to create arable land in a co-operative effort, creating the water boards that still manage water in the
  Netherlands". (Present-day rates 2-25 mm/yr: from secondary coverage of the paper; check in the PDF before use.)
- Canon van Nederland, Beemster: permission 1607 (Provincial Council of Holland and West Friesland), dry 1612;
  43 windmills; 38 km dyke with ring canal; Jan Adriaenszoon Leeghwater (1575-1650) co-responsible for dyke and mills;
  Amsterdam mayors and merchants; UNESCO 1999. https://www.canonvannederland.nl/en/beemster
- ASME landmark #153 Cruquius: one of three nearly identical steam pumping stations that drained the Haarlemmermeer
  1849-52 and kept it dry for 80+ years; Leeghwater's 1629 windmill plan never realised.
  https://www.asme.org/about-asme/engineering-history/landmarks/153-cruquius-pumping-station
- Lowest point: 6.76 m below NAP, Zuidplaspolder near Nieuwerkerk aan den IJssel (Wikipedia list of extreme points;
  say "below NAP / below sea level" loosely only with "about").
- Kinderdijk: mills built 1738 (Nederwaard, brick) and 1740 (Overwaard, wooden); UNESCO 1997 "Mill Network at
  Kinderdijk-Elshout"; 19 mills (count varies by definition: say "nineteen" only with the UNESCO wording); 1421 St
  Elizabeth flood drowned the Grote Hollandse Waard, the Alblasserwaard stayed dry. (Wikipedia; check UNESCO page.)
- 1953: Watersnoodmuseum: 1,836 people drowned in the Netherlands (873 in Zeeland, Zeeuwse Ankers); England and
  Scotland 326; ~200,000 ha under water, 72,000 evacuated (Louwerse, Watersnoodmuseum presentation); museum in the
  Ouwerkerk caissons, opened 2 April 2001. https://watersnoodmuseum.nl/en/stories/victims-the-flood
- Delta Works: Delta Committee set up 20 days after the flood; Oosterscheldekering opened 1986 (built 1960-1986);
  Maeslantkering 1997 (some sources: works complete 1997/1998; say "1997, the last big barrier").
- Zuiderzee Works: after the 1916 flood, under Cornelis Lely, 1919-1986; Afsluitdijk ~32 km, closed 1932 (some
  sources 1933 for the finished road: say 1932, the closure); Flevopolder NE half 1957, SW half 1968; Flevoland
  province 1986; total new land ~1,650 km2 (check with Rijkswaterstaat / Zuiderzee Museum before use).

## Facts read, round 2 (2026-11-16)
- Schiphol: about 3 m below NAP (Wikipedia infobox -3 m AMSL; Visit Holland "around 3 m below NAP"; Schiphol's own
  blog says about four metres): say "about three metres below sea level". On the bed of the Haarlemmermeer, drained
  1848/1849-1852. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol
- Haarlemmermeer growth: Flevolands Geheugen: grew by erosion and peat extraction from 9,100 ha (1250) to 16,850 ha
  (1848); Britannica: lakes of ~5,700 ha in 1531 merged by inundations, ~18,200 ha by the 1830s. Storms of 1836: 29 Nov
  ~4,000 ha flooded toward Amsterdam, 26 Dec ~7,500 ha toward Leiden (flevolandsgeheugen "Draining the
  Haarlemmermeer"). Cruquius Museum: erosion and peat digging merged several lakes into one. Polder ~18,000 ha.
- 1916: storm surge, night of 13-14 Jan 1916, Zuiderzee dike breaches; Zuiderzee law in force 14 June 1918 (Lely,
  minister); Rijkswaterstaat: last gap of the Afsluitdijk closed 28 May 1932, the sea became the IJsselmeer; new
  land 165,000 ha (~1,650 km2). https://www.rijkswaterstaat.nl/water/waterbeheer/bescherming-tegen-het-water/100-jaar-zuiderzeewet
- Oosterscheldekering: opened by Queen Beatrix 4 Oct 1986 ("the Delta Works are completed, Zeeland is safe"); 62
  gates; length ~9 km in total (ICE: nearly 8 km with 3 km open-close barrier: say "kilometres long", no number).
- Maeslantkering: built 1991-1997; two gates 210 m long held by 237 m trusses; closes when a surge above ~3 m is
  expected; closes in about 2 hours; first closed for a storm 8 Nov 2007; protects the Nieuwe Waterweg (360 m wide)
  to Rotterdam. (Wikipedia + Watersnoodmuseum "Maeslant Barrier").
- Room for the River: 2006-2015 (some projects to 2018-2022), 34 locations on IJssel, Rhine, Lek, Waal; Rhine
  design discharge raised 15,000 -> 16,000 m3/s after the 1993 and 1995 floods; Delta Programme plans for up to
  18,000 m3/s. https://english.deltaprogramma.nl/delta-programme/question-and-answer/what-about-the-18000-m3-river-discharge
- Peat meadows today: on average about 1 cm a year (Gooi en Vechtstreek report); western peat meadows could drop up
  to 120 cm by 2050 if water levels keep following the ground; PBL (letter to parliament June 2019): extra costs of
  subsidence to 2050 ~EUR 21 bn in cities + EUR 1 bn rural. Rli target 70 % less subsidence by 2050.
  https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-35141-3.html
- Our map layer (geo/make_regions.py): ETOPO 2022 60" cells below 0 m inside the Natural Earth country: 12,700-13,500
  km2, 34-36 % of the land polygon (coarse grid; at -0.5 m 28 %, at -1 m 23 %). Narration uses PBL's 26 %; the map is
  labelled "approx." and never quoted as a number.

## Facts read, round 3 (for length)
- Terps (wierden): dwelling mounds in Friesland/Groningen; mound building from about 500 BC (some sources 650 BC),
  some Frisian mounds up to 15 m (Wikipedia Terp; Univ. Groningen study). Say "more than two thousand years ago".
- NAP: in 1684 Amsterdam mayor Johannes Hudde had the water of the IJ measured for a year; the average summer high
  water became Amsterdams Peil; marble "Hudde stones" set in sluices; minimum sea-dike height 9 ft 5 in above AP;
  normalised 1885-1894 as Normaal Amsterdams Peil (Wikipedia Amsterdam Ordnance Datum; NOS 2018).
- Royal Palace, Dam Square (1648-1665, Jacob van Campen): stands on 13,659 wooden piles (Wikipedia Royal Palace of
  Amsterdam; mirrors, not independent): say "more than thirteen thousand wooden piles".
- Walcheren 1944: Allied bombers breached the sea dikes from 3 Oct 1944 (Operation Infatuate), island flooded,
  repairs from Oct 1945 (Wikipedia Inundation of Walcheren). Casualties differ (152/159): do not quote.
- Wieringermeer: German troops blew up the dike 17 Apr 1945; polder full in ~2 days; ~7,000 residents displaced;
  drained again by late 1945 (Wikipedia Inundation of the Wieringermeer).
- 1995: high water on Rhine/Waal/Maas; largest evacuation since WWII, about 250,000 people (voxweb.nl, Radboud);
  Rijkswaterstaat 2025 retrospective; led with 1993 to Room for the River.
  https://www.rijkswaterstaat.nl/en/news/archive/2025/01/thirty-years-later-the-1995-high-water-evacuation-remains-unforgettable
- Groningen gas: centre of the field had subsided ~33 cm by end 2013 (de Waal et al., PIAHS 2015); induced quakes
  since 1991 damaged houses. (End of production date not verified: do not say a year.)
- KNMI'23: sea level off the Dutch coast in 2100: 59-124 cm (high emissions), 26-73 cm (low), relative to the start of
  this century; up to ~2.5 m possible if the Antarctic ice sheet becomes unstable (worst case).
  https://www.knmi.nl/over-het-knmi/nieuws/knmi23klimaatscenario-s
- Sand Motor (Zandmotor): 21.5 million m3 of sand (19 M in the hook-shaped peninsula), built Mar-Nov 2011 between
  Kijkduin and Ter Heijde; the sea spreads it along the coast. https://dezandmotor.nl/en/about-the-sand-motor/
- 1953 Hollandse IJssel: at ~5:30 AM a ~15 m section of the dike near Nieuwerkerk/Ouderkerk aan den IJssel gave way;
  the grain barge De Twee Gebroeders (skipper Arie Evegroen), on the mayor's / dike reeve's order, was steered into the
  gap; sandbags behind it; the dike held. Canon van Nederland: "a ship is navigated into the breach, and as a result,
  the dyke holds" (prevented a far worse disaster). Popular figure "3 million kept dry": say "millions", attributed
  loosely. https://www.canonvannederland.nl/en/watersnood
- Ouwerkerk: the last breach closed 6 Nov 1953 with four Phoenix caissons built in Britain for the 1944 Mulberry
  harbours (not used in the war); now the Watersnoodmuseum (opened 2001; national monument 2003).
- Water boards: about 2,670 in 1953 merged to a few dozen (Springer 2016 table; 21 today per Unie van Waterschappen:
  not verified, say "a few dozen"); elections every four years (2023 Dutch water board elections, Wikipedia).
- Cruquius (ASME): Cornish-cycle compound engine, 5 strokes/min, eight lift pumps, ~55,000 US gallons a minute
  (~210 m3) raised about 15 ft; retired 1932, preserved as a monument.
- Eastern Scheldt: fishermen, oyster farmers and environmentalists (Oosterschelde study group) opposed a closed dam;
  1976 decision: a storm surge barrier closed only in storms, keeping the estuary salty and tidal; built Apr 1976-Jun
  1986 (Watersnoodmuseum "Eastern Scheldt Storm Surge Barrier"; Zeeuwse Ankers).
- Rli (Council for the Environment and Infrastructure) target: 70 % less peat subsidence by 2050 (kst-35141-3).
- 1995 peak at Lobith 16.68 m NAP (Rijkswaterstaat 2025 retrospective; voxweb 16.63): say "almost seventeen meters".
