# Olympic Peninsula: research (round 1, 2026-11-23)

Status: V = primary/official read; A = secondary (attribute or confirm); OWN = our calculation; CHECK = not yet confirmed.

## 1. Rain and rain shadow
- V (NPS "Temperate Rain Forests", updated 11 Apr 2025): "Precipitation in Olympic's rain forest ranges from 140 to 167
  inches (12 to 14 feet) every year." Valleys: Quinault, Queets, Hoh, Bogachiel. Sitka spruce, western hemlock; trees
  "can reach 250 feet in height and 30 to 60 feet in circumference"; temperature seldom below freezing.
- A (Wikipedia Sequim; NOAA 1991-2020 normals table): Sequim 16.85 in a year; "less than 16 inches ... about the same as
  Los Angeles"; lies "within the rain shadow of the Olympic Mountains". Population 8,024 (2020). CHECK NOAA normals directly.
- A (Wikipedia Olympic Mountains): Mount Olympus 7,980 ft; western slopes "the wettest place in the 48 contiguous states";
  ~184 glaciers, expected to largely disappear by 2070. A (Forks NOAA normals via Wikipedia): 119.94 in a year.

## 2. People and land
- A (Wikipedia, 2020 census): Clallam 77,155 on 1,738 sq mi land (44.4/sq mi); Jefferson 32,977 on 1,804 sq mi
  (18.3 OWN); King 2,269,675 (1,073/sq mi). Forks 3,335 (2020), "Logging Capital of the World", boom 1950s-70s, decline in

- A (Wikipedia Olympic NP): Mount Olympus National Monument 2 Mar 1909; national park 29 Jun 1938; 922,650 acres; coastal

  3,717,267 visitors (2024).

## 3. Elwha
- V (NPS Elwha restoration): Elwha and Glines Canyon dams "built in the early 1900s"; "the largest dam removal in U.S.
  history began on September 17, 2011"; Elwha Dam gone six months later, Glines Canyon in 2014; 11 kinds of salmon and trout.
- A (Wikipedia, citing WDFW and NOAA): dams 108 ft and 210 ft; Elwha Dam built 1910-1912 (operational 1913), Glines Canyon
  1927; ~392,000 fish a year before the dams, <3,000 late 20th century (WDFW 2010); 10.5 million metric tons of sediment
  released; ~$351.4 M. CHECK USGS/NOAA for sediment and returns since removal.

## 4. 26 January 1700
- A (Wikipedia, citing Atwater et al. 2005 USGS PP 1707 and Satake et al. 1996 Nature): ~9 pm PT, 26 Jan 1700; Mw 8.7-9.2;
  ~1,000 km rupture, ~20 m average slip; tsunami hit Japan on Genroku 12, 12th month, 8th-9th days (27-28 Jan 1700), waves
  2-5 m; ghost forests of red cedar (Copalis River, WA) with last rings from 1699; Makah story of a night-time quake,
  Quileute flood story. Recurrence ~500 yr average (13 events, 300-900 yr). 2010: 37 % chance of M8.2+ in 50 years,
  10-15 % for a full M9 rupture. CHECK USGS event page / PNSN; do not quote pubs.usgs.gov PDFs (fetch denied earlier).
