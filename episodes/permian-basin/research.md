## Facts read (source per line)
### Today
- Permian (W Texas + SE New Mexico) 6.6 M b/d in 2025 (6.3 in 2024), 48 % of U.S. crude; U.S. record 13.6 M b/d.
  EIA Today in Energy 67404: https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67404
  (do not mix with the separate 6.0 M b/d / 44 % Dec 2025 shale-formation figure, EIA 67364)
- USGS 2018 Delaware Basin: 46.3 billion bbl undiscovered technically recoverable continuous oil + 281 Tcf gas, largest
  continuous assessment ever (more than double the 2016 Midland Wolfcamp 20 bn). Not proved reserves.
  https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20183073 ; https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/usgs-identifies-largest-continuous-oil-and-gas-resource-potential-ever-assessed

### The sea and the reef
- Permian Basin was an inland sea joined to the ocean around Pangea by a narrow channel; Delaware Sea; Capitan Reef
  built 275-277 Ma (USGS). https://www.usgs.gov/geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks/geology-guadalupe-mountains-national-park
- Capitan Reef: mostly buried, ~400-mile U-shaped reef; longest exposed stretch ~40 mi from Guadalupe Mountains NP to
  near Carlsbad (12 mi in the park). NPS GUMO: https://www.nps.gov/gumo/whatssospecial.htm
- Reef up to 1,800 ft thick (NPS geodiversity atlas Carlsbad). https://www.nps.gov/articles/nps-geodiversity-atlas-carlsbad-caverns-national-park-new-mexico.htm
- Stagnant deep basin: dead organisms buried without oxygen -> heat and pressure -> oil and gas (USGS page above).
- Uplift began ~80 Ma; faulting exposed the reef (USGS page above).
- Guadalupe Peak 8,751 ft, highest in Texas; El Capitan cliff. TO CHECK on NPS before using.
- Carlsbad Cavern and Lechuguilla dissolved by sulfuric acid: H2S from the oil reservoirs rose (as late as ~12 Ma, USGS)
  and met oxygenated groundwater; gypsum is the evidence. Carlsbad Cavern >30 mi; Lechuguilla >138-140 mi (NPS).
  https://www.nps.gov/cave/learn/nature/cave.htm ; https://www.usgs.gov/science-support/osqi/yes/national-parks/geology-carlsbad-caverns-national-park

### The sea dies: salt
- WIPP salt deposited ~250 Ma as the Permian Sea evaporated (DOE); Salado Fm ~255 Ma, 655 m deep (Swift, Sandia);
  rooms 2,150 ft down; salt creeps and seals. https://wipp.energy.gov/wipp-site.asp ; https://www.energy.gov/em/articles/quick-facts-about-ems-waste-isolation-pilot-plant
- Salado 200-400 m thick, Castile below (National Academies). https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/5269/chapter/12

### First boom
- Santa Rita No. 1 came in 28 May 1923, ~13 mi west of Big Lake, Reagan County, on Permanent University Fund land:
  first big Permian find. First PUF royalty Aug 1923 ($516.53 vs $516.63: do not show). Big Lake field ~2.4 bn bbl?
  (one source; CHECK) ; plugged 1990; rig now on the UT Austin campus.
  https://utsystem.edu/board-of-regents/history/santa-rita ; https://atlas.thc.texas.gov/Details/5383004587
- PUF: ~2.1 million acres, mostly West Texas; market value $39.5 bn (30 Jun 2025, UTIMCO); minerals revenue
  $1.72 bn in FY2025 (University Lands annual report). https://www.utimco.org/media/4634/2025-06-puf-semi-annual.pdf
- Yates field, Pecos County, discovered late Oct 1926 (28 or 29: use "October 1926"); rated 72,869 b/d from 1,150 ft
  (THC marker); Iraan named after Ira and Ann Yates; >500 M bbl. https://atlas.thc.texas.gov/Details/5371003202
- Permian liquids peaked 1973 (~2.16 M b/d, IHS via BIC) then declined for decades (Dallas Fed).
  https://www.dallasfed.org/research/swe/2022/swe2201/swe2201a ; https://www.bicmagazine.com/industry/drilling-exploration/march-2018-permian-basin-reaches-new-production-peak-in-2017/
- 1980s bust: oil fell to ~$10/bbl; 425 Texas commercial banks failed 1980-89 incl. 9 of 10 largest holding companies (FDIC).
  https://www.fdic.gov/resources/publications/history-eighties/volume-1/history-80s-volume-1-part2-09.pdf
  Midland bank runs: Handbook of Texas via NPR (attributed; read the Handbook before using).

### Shale
- Rigs: 92 (May 2009) -> 525 (June 2012) (Dallas Fed); horizontal share 90 % 2018, 95 % 2022.
  https://www.dallasfed.org/research/energy11/permian
- 2017 passed the 1973 peak (~815 M bbl vs 790 M bbl; IHS Markit via BIC: attributed).

### The bill
- Produced water: ~20+ M b/d in 2024-25 (estimates 21 BEG, 25.3 TTU); ~2.6 bbl water per bbl oil Midland,
  ~5 Delaware (BEG). https://wrp.beg.utexas.edu/episode/assessing-potential-for-beneficial-use-of-produced-water-in-the-texas-permian-basin
- Injected deep -> earthquakes. RRC commissioner (2022 op-ed): injection into deeper formations likely responsible.
  M5.4 16 Nov 2022, Reeves/Culberson (Texas Tribune; CHECK USGS event page for location); M5.2 Nov 2023.
  RRC acts at M3.5+; Gardendale SRA disposal suspended Dec 2021. https://www.rrc.texas.gov/news/013122-commissioner-wright-op-ed
- Flaring seen by VIIRS (NOAA). Satellite estimate 2017: 104 bcf flared vs 55 reported (EDF: attributed).
  https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/revealing-the-night-sky-detecting-natural-gas-flares-the-visible-infrared-imaging-radiometer-suite-viirs
- Wink Sink 1: 3 June 1980, ~110 ft deep, Winkler County, above the Capitan reef; salt dissolution in the Salado;
  Johnson 1989: likely influenced by petroleum activity (Hendrick 10-A, drilled 1928, inside the rim). Wink Sink 2 (date
  to check, ~2002). https://www.beg.utexas.edu/publications/114 ; https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70015673

## Gate-1 outline (target ~2,750 words, 7 chapters, ~16 min at 2.75 w/s)
0. Cold open (~200): from orbit, a grid of thousands of white dots in a brown desert; almost nobody lives here, yet
   it pumps nearly half of America's oil. Why here? The answer is a sea that died 250 million years ago.
1. A sea at the edge of Pangea (~400): the inland sea, the narrow channel, the Delaware and Midland basins, the
   Central Basin Platform between them (painted map over today's satellite view).
2. The reef that became a mountain (~400): Capitan Reef, 400 miles around the basin; Guadalupe Peak, El Capitan;
   Carlsbad Caverns dissolved by acid that rose from the oil below.
3. The sea dies (~350): evaporation, salt hundreds of metres thick, the oil sealed in; today the U.S. buries nuclear
   waste in that salt (WIPP).
4. The first boom (~450): Santa Rita No. 1, 1923, on land given to the University of Texas; Yates 1926; Midland and
   Odessa; peak 1973, the long decline, the 1980s bust.
5. The second boom (~450): sideways drilling and fracking turn the source rock itself into the reservoir;
   rigs 92 -> 525; 2017 beats 1973; 6.6 M b/d, 48 %; USGS 46.3 bn bbl.
6. The bill (~400): water 2-5 barrels per barrel of oil, injected deep, earthquakes; flares seen from orbit;
   the Wink sinkholes in the salt.
7. Ending (~100): question to the viewer, no generic CTA ("How long should Texas keep pumping its dead sea?").
