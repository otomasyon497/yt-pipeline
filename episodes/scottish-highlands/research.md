## 2. Rock, ice, peat, rain
- Highland Boundary Fault Arran-Stonehaven (CHECK BGS). Glaciation scraped soil (CHECK BGS/NatureScot).
- V (RSPB / HES / UNESCO UK): Flow Country inscribed July 2024, world's first peatland World Heritage Site, site
  190,000 ha (1,900 km2) of ~400,000 ha blanket bog; RSPB: nearly 20 % drained and planted with forestry, >2,600 ha
  of plantation since removed. A (Wikipedia): 1970s-80s tax breaks drove the planting. CHECK the 1988 budget change.
- V (Met Office 1991-2020, Fort Augustus): ~1,359 mm a year. CHECK Fort William / Kinlochewe and Edinburgh pages.

## 3. People removed
- Culloden 16 Apr 1746 (CHECK NTS).
- A (Wikipedia Patrick Sellar / NLS timeline): Strathnaver notices Dec 1813, first removals 28 families (~140
  people), Badinloskin June 1814; Sellar tried at Inverness, acquitted 24 Apr 1816. ~15,000 cleared from the Sutherland
  estates 1811-21 (popular sources: attribute or CHECK Richards).
- Potato famine 1846; HIES 1852-57 ~5,000 to Australia (CHECK primary); Hector 1773 ~190 to Pictou (CHECK).
- A (Perlego excerpt, likely Devine): Highlands + far north 17 % of Scotland's people in 1831, 7 % in 1911. CHECK.
- V (Hansard 17 Apr 1893): deer forest returns 1,975,209 acres (1883) and 2,572,133 acres (1891).
- V (NTS): St Kilda's 36 islanders taken off at their own request on 29 Aug 1930 (HMS Harebell, to Morvern).

## 4. Norway contrast
- A (1961 article abstract, Paris 1900 publication): Norwegian tenants bought their farms from the late 1600s;
  freeholders "may have been a majority" by ~1750; odelsrett (allodial right) in the 1814 constitution. Attribute.
- CHECK: Norway's fisheries and hydro (first big hydro early 1900s) for the contrast.

## 5. Power and land today
- A (Wikipedia, Spectator 1954, Scotsman): Hydro-Electric Development (Scotland) Act 5 Aug 1943; NoSHEB motto "Neart nan
  glean" (power from the glens); 54 power stations, 78 dams, ~200 miles of tunnels; first scheme Morar Dec 1948.
- A (Andy Wightman, Who Owns Scotland 2024): 433 owners hold half of privately owned rural land (prelim; later 421;
  parliament note 408). Say "about four hundred, according to the land researcher Andy Wightman".
- A (isleofeigg.org, Scotsman): Eigg community buyout 12 Jun 1997 (65 residents). Assynt 1993, Land Reform (Scotland)
  Act 2003 community right to buy: CHECK.

# Gate-1 outline (draft, ~2,800 words, ~17.6 min)
Title: "Why Almost Nobody Lives in the Scottish Highlands" (48 chars). Cover: bright glen photo + "EMPTIER THAN NORWAY" (cover_photo).
0. Cold open: Highland council area bigger than Wales, ~235,000 people, 9 per km2. Across the North Sea, Vestland:
   same ancient rock, same Atlantic rain, twice the density and a city of 290,000. Question: why is one full and one empty?
1. Rock and ice: Highland Boundary Fault, glaciers, thin soil, peat; the Flow Country (and the 1980s conifer rush).
2. The rain wall: western rainfall; but Norway gets the same -> nature is not the whole answer.
3. Clans to cash: Culloden 1746, chiefs become landlords.
4. The Clearances: sheep, Strathnaver 1814, Sellar acquitted 1816, crofts on the coast; ruined townships on satellite.
5. Hunger and ships: 1846 blight, emigration to Canada and Australia; 17 % -> 7 % of Scotland's people.
6. Deer forests and St Kilda: 1.98 M -> 2.57 M acres for sport (1883-1891); St Kilda 1930.
7. Norway's different path: freeholders and odal right, fishing farmers; power from the glens (Hydro Board 1943) lit
   the Highlands but did not refill them.
8. Who owns it now: ~400 owners hold half the private rural land (Wightman); Assynt, Eigg, Land Reform 2003; holiday homes.
9. Ending question: "Should Scotland break up the big estates to bring people back to the glens?"
