## What they leave out (our room)
- Both say "why" in under 5 minutes, with the same 5 sentences (likely one source). No dates beyond 1828/1907, no people, no maps of their own.
- Nobody explains **how the cattle got there before the people** (Hernandarias's cattle, 1611-17, wild herds, the "vaquerías").
- Nobody tells the **buffer state** story: Britain's mediation (Ponsonby, 1828) and why a small state between Brazil and Argentina suited London.
- Nothing on **wire fences (1870s)** pushing gauchos off the land into rural shanty "pueblos de ratas"; nothing on the **Guerra Grande** (1839-51) or Salsipuedes (1831).
- No **comparison**: Rio Grande do Sul across the border has the same pampa grass and far more people. Why?
- No **own measurement**. We can count (Kontur population grid): share of Uruguayans within X km of Montevideo / the coast; area holding 90 % of people.
- No modern turn: forestry and pulp mills (UPM Fray Bentos, Paso de los Toros) changing the interior; 98 % renewable power; wind farms on the ranches.

## Our spine (draft)
Hook: bigger than England, 3.4 M people, more cows than people; half in one city. Then 5-6 reasons: cattle before people; buffer state with one port; wars and fences emptied the countryside; the immigrants went to Buenos Aires; families had fewer children first; and the people who left (1970s, 2002). Ending: forests, pulp and wind on the old ranches; does the interior fill now? Comment question.
No retiree-guide section (that is their angle; we stay geographic and historical).
