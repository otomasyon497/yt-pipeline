## What they leave out (our room)
- **The cattle kings and the Johnson County War (April 1892):** nobody tells how the Wyoming Stock Growers Association, the open range and British/Scottish capital kept the land in a few hands, the 1886-87 winter die-off, the invasion with hired Texas gunmen, the siege of the TA Ranch and the cavalry rescue. It is the "why" behind few small farms.
- **The checkerboard today:** Pin & Ponder shows the pattern, nobody tells the 2021 corner-crossing case (four Missouri hunters, a $7 M+ claim by the ranch owner, 10th Circuit March 2025, Supreme Court refused it 20 Oct 2025). A live story that comes straight out of the 1860s land grant.
- **Teapot Dome (1922):** Wyoming's oil was the centre of the biggest US scandal before Watergate; nobody uses it.
- **Gillette Syndrome (1974):** the boomtown named by a psychologist; coal from 466 Mt (2008) to ~190 Mt (2024).
- **Own measurement:** share of Wyomingites living within X km of the 1868 Union Pacific line (Kontur grid), and the counties with more pronghorn than people.
- **Who leaves:** Harvard Growth Lab 2024: nearly two thirds of Wyoming-born people have left by their thirties.
- **The future on the old range:** the Chokecherry-Sierra Madre wind project (~600 turbines, 3,550 MW, Carbon County, building since 2025) and TerraPower's Natrium reactor at Kemmerer (NRC permit March 2026, a coal town). Both need few hands: does anything fill Wyoming?

## Our spine (draft)
Hook: bigger than the UK, fewer people than Washington DC; one in three counties ... (to measure). Reasons: (1) too high and too dry, a road not a destination (trails, South Pass); (2) the railroad drew the towns and the checkerboard (and the 2025 case); (3) the cattle kings closed the range (1886-87, Johnson County War); (4) the government kept half the land (Yellowstone 1872, Taylor Grazing Act, Jackson); (5) boom and bust underground (Teapot Dome, uranium, Gillette, coal decline); (6) the children leave. Ending: wind and Natrium; question.
Avoid: RealLifeLore's Colorado frame as the spine (use one short comparison), travelogue, listicle of ghost towns.
